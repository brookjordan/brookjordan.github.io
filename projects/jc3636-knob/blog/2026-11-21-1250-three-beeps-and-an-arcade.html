Three beeps at once, and an arcade

2026-11-21 12:50 (Pacific) · part 12 of 21 laboccamysoundarcade

kitchen-timer was the first game under the new runner, and it was a joy: built in 20 minutes, two replies cut off and both auto-resumed, its own feature scripts written, CHECK PASS. Then the coach listened to the alarm. Well. "Listened".

Three beeps, one millisecond

The brief asked for three alarm beeps when the timer hits zero. The event log said three beeps. All three started in the same millisecond.

On the board, the sound code mixes voices. Three identical beeps at the same instant aren't three beeps, they're one beep, louder and probably clipped. The simulator logged three sounds. Your ears would have heard one, with a crunch.

One symptom note later (three minutes and a half!), they were at 60,583, 60,984 and 61,384 ms, about 400 ms apart, with a new feature-script line that proves it. Beep. Beep. Beep. Perfect.

The arcade

Flashing the knob means holding BOOT and pressing RESET, which needs me, so flashing one game at a time was going to get old fast. Hence tools/arcade.py: give it a list of finished games and it builds one firmware with all of them.

The trick is lovely and slightly evil. Each game's main.c is copied unchanged, with a two-line header on top that renames its app_main and routes its setup calls and its screen flush through a little arcade layer. Setup runs once, however many games ask for it. And holding the screen for 3 seconds inside any game jumps straight back to the menu, from inside the flush, with a longjmp. The games don't know they're in an arcade. They just suddenly aren't running any more.

At 12:50 the arcade held four games and passed its own check. Knob picks, tap plays, hold to come home. A tiny cabinet of tiny games.
