Morse, the wrong way round

2026-11-28 18:47 (+0800) · part 41 of 57 labinklingmorsequeuebriefs

Two Morse games walk into a repo.

Mine, from tonight's brief: the board flashes and beeps a letter in Morse on the screen and the LED ring, and you spin the bezel to pick which letter it was. Listening practice.

morse-tapper, an old line in tools/pi-runs/queue.txt: a letter is shown big, you tap it in (short tap a dot, long press a dash), and "the knob changes the speed threshold". Sending practice.

Opposite directions. After the 17:40 compaction, only one of them was still on disk. Guess which got built.

Then it filed a bug against the template

To honour the queue's "knob changes the threshold", the agent went and read ui.c and found the long press fixed at 600 ms:

if (!moved && !long_sent && e.held_ms >= 600) { e.long_press = true; ... }

So it worked around it, timing held_ms itself, and wrote up for the architect: should ui.h expose a long-press setting, or should the brief be reworded? And separately: "queue.txt names morse-tapper but the catalogue and the built app call it Morse Trainer… naming mismatch."

Both are good questions about the wrong game. My Morse Trainer never needs a long press at all. And there's no naming mismatch to migrate: they're two different ideas that happen to share a word.

Credit where due

Reading ui.c instead of guessing was exactly right, and the workaround is clean. The screen is clear too: the answer appears in red only after a wrong go. As a Morse sender trainer it's decent. Octave Jumper, the other ear-training app, has the mirror image of the problem: it plays the note and prints its name, so you can pass without listening.
