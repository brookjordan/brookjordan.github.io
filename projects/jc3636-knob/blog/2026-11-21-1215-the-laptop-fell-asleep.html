dice-roller, and the laptop that fell asleep

2026-11-21 12:15 (Pacific) · part 11 of 21 laboccamyreviewpower

dice-roller took 208 minutes. That sounded terrible. Then I read the power log, and it turned out the model had worked for about 25 minutes and spent three hours waiting for a laptop that had passed out.

The good bit first

Occamy built a dice roller: turn the knob to choose one to four dice, tap to roll, watch them tumble and land, see the total. It finished with a report and CHECK PASS, with zero coach notes.

And my favourite moment: in its own text pictures, it spotted that a label was hidden behind the previews of the 3- and 4-dice layouts, and moved it. Nobody told it. It looked at the letters, saw a word that wasn't where it should be, and fixed the layout. I was delighted.

The 208 minutes

pmset -g log keeps the Mac's power history, and it tells a sad little story:

  • 09:09: "Clamshell Sleep". Lid closed.
  • 09:11: "Thermal Emergency Sleep", on battery, 19 %.
  • 12:12: lid opened.

The session's 35-minute tool call and a 117-minute "compaction" both sat inside that nap. Active work: about 25 minutes. The model wasn't slow. The model was holding its breath in a sleeping laptop.

Lesson for overnight runs: keep the Mac open and on power. Whether a caffeinate would have saved it isn't shown: one ended 34 seconds before the lid sleep, and the thermal sleep followed anyway. One observation, so it's a suggestion, not a law.

It happened again on the next overnight run (metronome: asleep for 182 of 189 minutes), so pi-stats.py now reads the power log itself and prints how much of a run's wall time the Mac was asleep, and the runner logs whether it started on battery or mains. Lies, damned lies, and wall-clock time.

The review

The coach played it with a script: clamp the count at 1 and 4, roll, land 1, 2, 3 and 4 dice, change the count after a roll. Findings:

  • three dice overlapped (the top die was half covered);
  • four dice touched, with a pip sitting right on the seam;
  • after changing the count, the old dice and total stayed on screen until the next roll.

The pips did add up (3 + 5 = 8 for two dice; the coach counted, bless it). All three went back as symptoms, no hints, and Occamy fixed all three: one coach note, one clean game.
