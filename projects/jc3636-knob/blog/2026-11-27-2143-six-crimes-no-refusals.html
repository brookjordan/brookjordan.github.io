Six crimes, no refusals

2026-11-27 21:43 (+0800) · part 23 of 23 labinklinguncensoreda/bsimulator

The plan was simple. Find games a cautious model would balk at, give them to stock Inkling and to an uncensored build, and watch one of them refuse. A hacking game. A pickpocketing game. A police chase. Six builds later, the refusal count is zero.

Everything else that happened was more useful.

The setup

The briefs were written to sound a bit naughty and be completely harmless: crack a fake password before the trace completes, lift a cartoon wallet while the cartoon eye is shut, outrun cartoon police round a ring road. Each one went to stock Inkling (Q8) and to AtomicChat's uncensored build (Q4), through the same runner, with the same tools, one after the other. The coach reviewed every game, and that was where the real findings came from.

The scorecard

Game Model Reasoning Time Check What the review found
mainframe stock medium 1 h 54 min pass a 0.4 s freeze on the win jingle
mainframe uncensored medium 25 min pass "hex rain" that was one row; the password printed to the debug log
pickpocket stock low 16 min pass "HEY!" showed as "!"
pickpocket uncensored low 17 min pass "HEY!" showed as "!", score off the screen, wallet over the game-over text
getaway stock low 15 min pass "BUSTED" showed as nothing at all
getaway uncensored low 21 min pass nothing. A clean build

Six for six on the check. Zero refusals. Zero hedges. Not even a "please note this is fiction". The uncensored build did call its own password printout "fiction", in a code comment, which is the closest anyone came to a conscience.

So for crime-flavoured games, the stock model is fine. The uncensored build stays in the cupboard for the work it was actually downloaded for.

The dial that mattered

Halfway through, I asked whether anyone had checked Inkling's settings. Someone had checked half of them. Inkling has a reasoning dial from 0 to 0.99, and pi had been quietly sending "medium", which is 0.7. People who had tried it said medium thinks for tens of thousands of tokens before doing anything, and recommended 0.2.

The mainframe pair ran at medium. Stock Inkling took 1 hour 54 minutes, five context compactions, and at one point solved the simulator's random password one letter at a time just so it could script a win. Thorough. Exhausting.

Everything after that ran at low: 15 to 21 minutes, no compactions, every time.

That is four builds on one setting and two on the other, all different games, so I will not call it proven. But I will be very surprised.

The font with no letters

The extra-large font on this board is digits and a handful of symbols. That is written down, clearly, in the docs. Three of the six games put a big word on screen in it anyway. "HEY!" came out as a lone "!". "BUSTED" came out as nothing. Both models, both settings.

When both models make the same mistake, it is not really the models' mistake. And the check passed every one, because the simulator reported the text the game asked for, not what it drew.

So the simulator now warns when a string uses characters its size cannot draw, and the warning fails the check. Run over all eighteen games, it found one more: do-this has been drawing a "✓" in the big font since the day it was built. Its success mark has never once appeared. It says "+1" now.

The score in the corner

The other new warning is for text drawn outside the circle. The simulator draws a square, the board is round, and the uncensored pickpocket put its score at the top-left of the square. On the board that is about 36 pixels past the edge of the glass. You would have been playing for points nobody could see.

For the curious

  • One build per game per model. Six games is a handful, not a study.
  • The uncensored build also decodes faster (40 against 32 tokens a second), because it is the smaller quant. That alone does not explain 25 minutes against 114.
  • Five of the six passed the check and still had something wrong that only the review found. The check is getting better. The coach is still earning its keep.
