The knob gets a keyboard

2026-11-27 19:07 (+0800) · part 22 of 23 ideasmusicsynthesismidi

A 49-key keyboard for a screen with one knob. This feels like a generous allocation of keys.

Dial Synth started with sounds under one finger. Then came the theremin. The Launchkey gives the next instrument a whole keybed, which means I can ask it to do something truly ambitious. Play a chord.

Pocket Synth

Open it and play. The bezel changes volume, a tap on Patch lets it choose a sound, and a few touch pads let me try the sound with the keyboard unplugged. Chords are the first milestone. Arpeggios can wait their turn.

The little board already has a speaker and a headphone jack. The headphone-only mode was checked on this unit, with the speaker quiet. So the first rig can be the knob, the Launchkey, a powered USB hub and headphones.

MIDI Bridge

Sometimes the sound should come from another box. Open MIDI Bridge and the first thing on screen is the route: keyboard in, chosen synth or computer out. Turn to pick the destination, tap to connect, play a note and see whether it went anywhere. That screen has a job of its own, especially when pairing goes sideways and everyone suddenly becomes very interested in the cable.

The same keyboard input could also give Piano Coach note presses and releases for Quick Draw and Rhythm. The acoustic piano still gets its own microphone test.

Piano Coach, Staff Sight, Echo Keys and Chord Spotter are all planned with a left- or right-handed setting for the knob. Pocket Synth and MIDI Bridge get it too. The touch controls move to the chosen side while the notes, stave and dial direction stay put. You still choose which hand to play the piano with.

First, release the key

Can the knob stay powered, recognise the Launchkey and see both ends of a note? That's the first bench check. On the MK4, a key release arrives as a Note On with zero velocity. Getting that wrong would make for a very long first chord. Novation's Launchkey MK4 programmer's reference

Pocket Synth and MIDI Bridge are in the Music & learning section of the knob catalogue. The Launchkey pairing and the artwork are still on the bench list.
