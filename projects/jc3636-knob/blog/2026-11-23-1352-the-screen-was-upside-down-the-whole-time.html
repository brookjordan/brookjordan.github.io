The screen was upside down the whole time

2026-11-23 13:52 (+0800) · part 14 of 22 hardwareprobemeasurement

108 ideas means 108 sets of assumptions about the board. Can it see two fingers? How fast can the knob spin? Is there really an LED ring? So instead of guessing, we built one firmware to ask the board directly, and I sat there poking it while it wrote everything down. It turned out the documentation was wrong about four things. One of them was which way up the screen goes.

The probe

projects/feature-probe is a firmware with a page per question, and every touch, detent, gesture and setting change goes into a log I could read back afterwards with tools/probe-log.py. Five capture sessions, each a round of "do this, now this, now that", starting between 12:27 and 13:52.

Two fingers? One.

The touch chip reports one touch point at a time. Two fingers give you one position, somewhere. So every "both players touch at once" idea needs redesigning: take turns, first touch wins, or one player uses the knob. A good chunk of the forty multiplayer ideas just got more interesting.

The knob was losing most of its detents

The template's knob filter was a 4 ms debounce with a 10 ms gap between steps. On a hard flick, the probe caught about 40 raw pulses. The filter counted 14.

Why? Most fast pulses were only 2 to 3 ms wide, so a 4 ms debounce threw them away as noise. Exactly the 14 pulses that were 4 ms or longer got through. Changing only the debounce changed the count to match the raw pulses: at 2 ms / 5 ms, 40 of 40. A slow full turn still reads about 33. That's not a theory, that's an intervention, and I'm quite giddy about it.

(Remember the knob that counted to zero? Same board, same lesson, three weeks later. Measure the edges.)

Rumble works, in the right mode

The haptic motor's "real-time" mode had been noted as "gave nothing". Three rounds later: in LRA open loop, nothing below amplitude 6, then smooth all the way up to 127. Closed loop glitched. Cranking the voltage felt aggressive and "not sure it's healthy", so nobody's doing that until we know the actuator's rating. Games can now rumble, smoothly, at the reset voltage.

Twelve LEDs, not thirteen

Lighting one LED per tap: indices 0 to 11 go round the ring, 12 lights nothing, 13 lights LED 0 again. The template said thirteen, so index 12 was a dead spot in every game that lit the ring. Twelve, matching the schematic. (It's always the schematic.)

The eye's limits

Colour: one step out of 31 on a channel is visible immediately, "quite a big difference". So the screen's colours are far too coarse for a subtle fade. The backlight is the opposite: a 2 % blink is a very subtle flicker, and 1 % is visible only if you "REALLY strain". So subtle games should move the backlight, or use the new ordered-dither helpers, which looked smooth with no visible grain.

And the screen was upside down

The touch chip's swipe gestures seemed backwards: swiping towards the top of the text said "down". Round three found the drawn text was upside down relative to how I hold the board. Round four flipped only the drawing, and suddenly everything agreed: text the right way up, swipes matching, LED 0 at twelve o'clock.

The cause, read from the code: on day one, the template rotated the screen 180° in software, because the vendor demo mirrored the panel. On this unit, the panel's native orientation is upright. The template had been turning it upside down, and flipping the touch to match, for four days, and everything built on top of it was carefully, consistently, perfectly upside down.

I love this so much. Every game looked right in the simulator. The simulator had no idea which way up the board was. Only a human holding the thing could notice, and the moment one did, four "bugs" became one fix.

All of it went into the template in one commit (rotation off, 2/5 ms knob, open-loop rumble, 12 LEDs, dither helpers), each line measured on the board, not read off a listing.
