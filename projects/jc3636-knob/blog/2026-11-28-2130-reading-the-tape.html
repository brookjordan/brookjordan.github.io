Reading the tape: how to review a run you didn't watch

2026-11-28 21:30 (+0800) · part 50 of 57 labreviewpimethod

The agent's last message at 21:13 was tidy: two documents, 21 passes, ready for the architect. I asked the coach (Claude) to review it. This post is the method, because it found things no summary would have.

1. Find the tape

tools/pi.sh keeps pi's sessions inside the repo, in tools/pi-agent/sessions/, not in pi's usual home-folder location. Tonight's is 908 KB, 579 events: 223 assistant turns, 221 tool results, 2 messages from me, 6 compactions.

2. Read what I said, then what the compactions said

The compaction entries keep their summaries. Reading the first one next to my original message is how the whole story fell out: "not repeated here". No amount of reading the final report would show that; the final report was written from those summaries.

3. Trace, then count

tools/pi-trace.py gives one line per tool call. Then a short script counted the boring things: when each new-game.sh ran (time per app), how many check.sh runs each app needed, how many results contained No such file or directory (22). Counting turns impressions into numbers you can check.

4. Look at every picture

All 46 feature shots, side by side, three contact sheets. Eight don't show their feature. You only see that by looking.

5. Check each claim against the thing it's about

Every "the doc doesn't say" got a grep of the doc. Every "silently skipped" got a read of the script. Every "needs replug" got a read of the skill. About half survived.

What it cost

Under an hour of reading. What it bought: the difference between "21 apps done, please review the long-press API" and "9 apps need rebuilding from the real briefs, and here's the one-line process change that stops it happening again". Tonight's other twenty-four posts are what came out of it, and one more lesson: reviewing the run is a skill the lab should write down, so the next review starts at step 1 instead of rediscovering where pi keeps its tapes.
