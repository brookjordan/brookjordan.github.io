The knob wants to teach me piano

2026-11-27 18:45 (+0800) · part 22 of 26 ideaslearningmusicpianomicrophone

Confession: I am struggling to learn the note names on a piano. So the next batch of ideas for the knob is not just games. It is a coach.

Everything on this board so far has been for fun: a theremin, a safe to crack, a pet that goes brown. All good. But a round screen that can listen, sitting on the music stand, is a very patient teacher. It does not sigh. It does not check its watch. It just times you, to the millisecond, which is worse.

Piano Coach

Two modes, both mine.

Quick draw. A note name appears, big: F♯, with a real sharp sign, not a hash. I play it, in any octave. The board hears it and shows how long that took. Then the next one. After twenty notes it shows my average, my best, and my three slowest notes, which is the bit that teaches. Whatever is on that list is what I practise next.

You choose which spellings come up, because the black keys have two names each: always sharps, always flats, white keys only, or all of it mixed.

Rhythm. A steady beat, and a scale to play on it. Every note lands as a dot on the rim, slightly before or after the tick, so I can see whether I rush or drag, and by how many milliseconds.

The knob does the settings: mode, sharps or flats, scale (major, the minors, chromatic, pentatonic, blues) and key. Turn to a setting, tap, turn, tap. No menus, no buttons, which is just as well, because there aren't any.

Four more

While we were at it:

Idea What it trains Needs the piano?
Staff Sight reading a note on the stave, not a letter yes
Echo Keys the board plays a phrase, you play it back yes
Chord Spotter "C minor. Go." Every note at once yes
Gap Finder hear two notes, name the interval with the knob no

Gap Finder needs no microphone at all, so it may well come first.

The catch

None of this works unless the board can hear my piano. It has a microphone, a tiny one, and nobody has ever turned it on. For the curious: its datasheet says it hears from about 100 Hz up, so the bottom two octaves of a piano arrive quiet.

There is one piece of luck. Working out which note a piano is playing is hard; people get the octave wrong all the time. But a trainer always knows which note it asked for. It only has to answer "was that an F♯?", in any octave. Octave errors stop mattering.

So the first job is a real test, me at the piano, playing C2 to C7 at the board and counting what it gets right. The ticket for that is written. Until it passes, these are ideas.

But they are the first ideas on this board that I need, rather than want. That feels like a promotion.
