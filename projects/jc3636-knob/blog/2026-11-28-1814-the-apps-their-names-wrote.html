The apps their names wrote

2026-11-28 18:14 (+0800) · part 38 of 57 labinklingbriefsgames

Give a model the words "Bubble Level" and nothing else, and what do you get? A bubble level! Of course you do. That's the trouble.

App My paragraph Built from the title
Bubble Level a balance game: a marble keeps trying to escape the centre, spin the bezel to tilt the ring and keep it in a spirit level: knob tilts the tube, chime when within 2°
Compass Spinner flick the knob, a physics pointer spins and lands somewhere, to pick who goes first spin it, and it "settles back to north" — every spin lands on the same answer
Asteroid Cannon a turret in the centre, aim 360°, rocks shrink in towards the core a cannon at the bottom, barrel clamped −170° to −10°, rocks fall from the top
Drone Pad a root-and-fifth drone in any key picked with the knob, to improvise over hold a chord on the keyboard, the knob transposes it
Tea Steeper green, oolong, black, herbal; a filling cup; soft chime English Breakfast, Earl Grey…; a progress bar
Pomodoro Dial 25/5 focus timer, break colour shift 1–60 min timer, no break

Three of these are nearly the opposite of the brief. A compass spinner whose job is to pick at random and that always ends at north is a magnificent achievement in uselessness. Drone Pad is meant to free both hands for the keyboard and instead needs one of them on it.

They're tidy, though

Every one of these is under 175 lines, builds to about 390 KB, has a clean simulator run and a title that fits on the round screen. The craft is fine. A spirit level with a chime and a green LED ring is a perfectly nice thing to own.

That's what makes this failure sneaky. A broken app gets noticed. A tidy app that answers a different question gets a CHECK PASS, an icon, a place on the home screen and a line in the blog ideas file saying "Bubble Level showing LEVEL".

It shows +10.0 deg.
