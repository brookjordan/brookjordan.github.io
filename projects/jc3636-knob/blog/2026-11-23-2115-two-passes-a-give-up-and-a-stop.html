The resume prompt earns its keep

Earlier that day, do-this run 2 died the way Occamy likes to die: three replies in a row cut off at the 32,768-token output cap, every one of them still planning, and not a line of game code. So the runner got a new resume prompt for exactly that case. In short: your plan is lost, write the file now.

Run 3 was its first outing. It hit the cap while planning at 20:03. Then again at 20:14. Each time the runner sent the new prompt, and after the second one there were no more cut-offs. Reported at 20:48, CHECK PASS fourteen seconds later. Fifty-five minutes, and the brief that had sunk two earlier runs was done.

Glorious. Mostly.

safe-cracker2: two hours and ten compactions

A port of the old safe-cracker onto the game template. It took 2 hours 8 minutes, compacted its context ten times, peaked just under 100,000 tokens, and passed. That is a lot of forgetting for one game. It also passed, which is the part the queue cares about.

The theremin

Then the theremin. Cut at the cap while planning at 20:55. Resumed with the new prompt. Cut again at 21:02. Resumed again. Cut a third time at 21:10, and the runner gave up, as it is built to after three. Twenty-two minutes, about 50,000 tokens of thinking each time, and no game.

The same prompt that saved do-this an hour earlier did nothing here. Hold that thought: on 27 November a different model gets this brief, and the prediction post bets on how that goes.

And tally-counter

Started at 21:10. Stopped by a SIGTERM to its runner at 21:15. The log records the signal and not who sent it or why, so neither will I.

What nobody has done yet

Nobody has reviewed safe-cracker2 or do-this. Both passed check.sh, and the pocket-pet post showed what that is worth on its own: seven green feature scripts and a brown pet. They are still uncommitted, waiting for a coach and, ideally, the board.
