pocket-pet passes, and the pet is brown

2026-11-23 16:03 (+0800) · part 16 of 17 laboccamystress testreview

pocket-pet was the stress test: the first brief with three timers running at once, a tap that means "feed" when awake and "wake up" when asleep, a long press competing with a tap, a spin competing with a click, and a mood made out of all of it. The brief was deliberately thin, just the queue line, no design doc. And the rule for the coach: no notes until it reports. Let's see where it breaks.

It didn't break

39 minutes. One reply cut off and auto-resumed. Three context compactions. Zero coach notes. Seven feature scripts, all passing. CHECK PASS. A virtual pet, with three need bars draining around the rim, a face that changes with its mood, munching, giggling, snoring and whimpering, built from one sentence by a model on my laptop.

And then the coach actually looked at it.

Four things the check didn't catch

1. The pet was brown. It's meant to be orange when happy. The colour eases towards its target a little each frame with the template's mix(), and mix() rounded down. Once each colour channel got close, 12 % of the gap rounded to nothing and the pet just... stopped, short, forever. Brown instead of orange, olive instead of grey-blue. The funniest part: the model had measured the brown itself, written "expect orange", got rgb(200,144,80), and ticked it. ✓. Very confident tick.

2. The age text left crumbs. "0 min" becomes "1 min", which is narrower, and the erase only covered the new width. Slivers of the old "0" and "n" stayed.

Enlarged crops. Left, first build: see the bits of "0" before the 1 and of "n" after it. Right, fixed.

3. The needs drained in 30 seconds, not the brief's "about 2 minutes". The brief said "about 2 minutes, for testing", and the model read "for testing" as permission to make it faster. That one's on the brief.

4. The pet's eraser chased the wrong pet. It erased at the pet's new position, not where it was last drawn. Invisible for now, because the bounce is small, but a bug waiting for a bigger bounce.

One note, three minutes

All four went back in one coach note, with what was wrong and how to check it. Occamy fixed all four in three minutes. The coach checked the shots itself: awake pet exactly the orange it should be, sad pet properly grey-blue, "1 min" clean, an 8-pixel bounce right there in its shot.

And the brown? That one's fixed for every future game too: mix() now rounds properly, and the docs show the one-line trick for easing a colour without stalling.

Meanwhile, do-this thought itself to death

The Do This! brief (click left, click right, spin left, spin right, tap) got two attempts. Run 2 gave up: every single reply hit the 32,768-token output limit while still thinking. The longest thought was 110,890 characters. Twelve tool calls. Zero lines of game code.

It wasn't looping; under 1 % of the paragraphs repeat. It was deliberating: drawing the spin-arrow icon in its head, arc by arc, and having a genuine philosophical crisis about whether a click 300 ms after another click counts (< or <=?), and each time it was resumed, it started planning again from the top.

I find this enormously relatable.

The candidate fixes: when a reply is cut off mid-thought, resume with "write the file now, then refine"; and a skill rule that the first version goes into a file in the first reply. Over-planning is now on the list of things the model should learn to warn about when a brief is probably beyond it.
