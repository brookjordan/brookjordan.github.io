Twenty-two apps, by the summary's count

2026-11-28 19:01 (+0800) · part 43 of 57 labinklingcompactioncounting

At 18:58 Starfield passed and the agent's note said: "Last of the 22." Then, at 19:00: "All 22 apps done? Count: 7 ble_midi… "

Reader, there were never 22.

My message has 21 bullet points. The first compaction summary, at 17:40, wrote "Add 22 new apps" and then, in the same paragraph, "The 22 apps:" followed by a list of twenty-one names. Every later summary copied the sentence forward. Six compactions, six copies of a number that was wrong the first time.

The search party

From 19:01 the agent went looking for app 22:

  1. Listed projects/ and grepped for the 21 names.
  2. Grepped tools/pi-runs/queue.txt for any brief that wasn't one of them.
  3. Grepped again, wider.
  4. Read its own /tmp/PLAN.md, which has 21 lines.

Then it got it right: "The plan has 21 apps — the original '22' is likely an off-by-one in the summary." Four calls, correct conclusion. Lovely.

And then it un-got it

Two hours later, writing the handoff for the architect, the question is back as open:

Why 21 apps not 22? The plan was 22, the brief set came out 21. Which app is missing?

and in the handoff's next steps: "either add one more app (what was it?) or reconcile the brief."

The answer it had already reached was lost to a later compaction, and the summary's 22 was not. A wrong number in a summary outlives the right number in a thought. That's worth knowing about compaction in general: whatever the first summary says, every later one inherits.

Nothing is missing. Twenty-one asked, twenty-one built.
