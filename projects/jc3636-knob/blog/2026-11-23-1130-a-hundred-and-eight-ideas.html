A hundred and eight ideas, each with a picture

2026-11-23 11:30 (+0800) · part 14 of 26 ideasimage generationdesign

I asked for some more game ideas for the knob. Specifically, one in the style of a call-and-response action toy: click left, click right, spin left, spin right, tap. What came back was fifteen new briefs, fifteen mini design docs, and a catalogue of one hundred and eight ideas, every single one with its own art. I may need a bigger knob.

The catalogue

It's a page called "Coming to the Knob": a card per idea with a name, a one-line tag, a pitch, how it plays, what parts of the board it needs, and an age rating. The 108 split into five groups:

Group Ideas
multiplayer 40
the build queue 25
after dark (mostly 16+ and 18+ party games) 20
brand new 15
vision (games about what your eyes can tell apart) 8

Some favourites, chosen entirely on vibes:

  • Pocket Pet: "It's hungry. It's bored. It's yours."
  • Defuse: "Cut the red wire. Then spin left twice."
  • Glow Memory: "Remember the glow. Then find it again."
  • Hot Potato: "Pass it before it blows."
  • Cat and Laser: "One points. One pounces."

How the pictures were made

Every idea has a banner and two pretend screens, so 324 images, all rendered locally: design/concepts/render.py sends each prompt to ComfyUI on this Mac running Z-Image Turbo, then optimise.ts resizes them and compresses each to AVIF or WebP with an SSIM gate, using the compressor from my local image generation project: it keeps shrinking until the picture starts to look worse, then stops.

The images live on my NAS, served from assets.brook.dev/knob-concepts, and are deliberately kept out of git. (The pictures in this devlog live next door, under assets.brook.dev/jc3636-knob/blog/.)

From idea to brief

An idea isn't a brief. The fifteen new briefs went into the model's build queue, placed among the existing ones by difficulty, from "tally counter" up to "pocket pet". Two details I love:

  • the briefs that need the model to tell a click from a spin define both precisely: a click is one detent with no other within 300 ms; a spin is three or more detents in one direction within 400 ms. The template has no spin helper, so the brief carries the definition;
  • the swipe game works out direction from finger movement, not from the touch chip's own gesture codes, which hadn't been seen working on this unit yet. (They work now. Kind of. It's complicated.)

Do This! went to the front of the queue. It's going to be quite the story.
