Seven piano apps before lunch, and a keyboard turned back to front

2026-11-28 11:10 (+0800) · part 28 of 57 labinklingpianomidileft-handed

"I'd love if the next apps it worked on were the ones for modifying the piano. Especially the ones that have a feature to make it play left handed!"

That is where this whole music tangent actually began. Not with a synth, not with a bridge, not even with note names. With wanting to play a left-handed piano: high notes on the left, low notes on the right, reflected around D4 (MIDI 62) so every white key lands on a white key and every black key lands on a black key.

By 11:03, Inkling 2 Mini Q8 (thinking low) had built all seven apps from the piano coach post and the keyboard post before breakfast was even on the plate.

Two kinds of left-handedness, and one immortal note

It turned out "left-handed" meant two different things that both mattered:

  1. Mirroring the piano keys (LH KEYS / LH ON). In pocket-synth and midi-bridge, every incoming or auditioned note reflects around D4 (124 - note), turning C4 into E4, D4 into D4, and C5 into E3. In midi-bridge's first draft, Note On got mirrored while Note Off (vel == 0) went straight through untransformed, which would have left every mirrored note ringing forever on the M-VAVE FM-1! The fix tracks active_out[128] per input key, so even if you flip the mirror switch mid-chord, releasing a key turns off the exact note that was turned on.
  2. Flipping the knob's touch controls (HAND / LH / RH). Moving primary tap targets to whichever hand is free while leaving the notes, stave, routing arrow and knob direction alone.

As covered in the companion coaching post, once we stopped micromanaging prompts and handed Inkling the raw catalogue pitches, it invented its own house style across the five learning apps. echo-keys built a vertical 12-semitone chromatic strip that jumps cleanly left to right on a tap; chord-spotter built a 3x4 circular pad grid that lights heard chord tones green, missing notes gold, and wrong thirds red!

The student caught a bug in the teacher's header

My favourite moment of the morning came from echo-keys's final report. It dutifully noted that projects/game-template/main/leds.h carried a warning saying the LED ring was "untested on this unit", so it warned us not to expect visible LEDs on hardware.

Reader, we counted all 12 LEDs glowing on the board four days ago! Line 1 of leds.h already said counted on the board, KNOB-039, and line 3 still said Untested on this unit right underneath it. Nobody had noticed until a local 30-tok/s model read the header properly and called it out. Fixed in f49617d.

Alternatives rejected

  • Waiting for USB Host and BLE MIDI drivers before building any piano UI. Next! Every app compiles against the real ESP-IDF template today with clean note hooks (midi_note_event, midi_bridge_in/out), so the UI, ergonomics and left-handed mirror logic are already proven in the simulator.
  • Mirroring around C4 (MIDI 60). Next! Reflecting around C maps black keys onto white keys (C# lands on B). D4 (MIDI 62) preserves the exact white-and-black key geometry.

What still hurts

Every one of these seven apps is simulator plus board build only:

  1. No live MIDI or microphone input wired yet. pocket-synth and midi-bridge still wait on the physical Launchkey 49 + powered USB 2.0 hub + M-VAVE FM-1 bench qualification (KNOB-056), and the five trainers still wait on either the PDM microphone piano test (KNOB-053) or BLE/USB MIDI pickup (KNOB-062).
  2. Real hardware feel. Touch accuracy on echo-keys's 12-row strip, speaker loudness vs headphones, and real left-handed playing ergonomics can only be judged once the firmware lands on the desk.
  • Banner concept (pending ComfyUI render once the queue frees the GPU, seed 4128): a miniature piano keyboard reflected in a glowing brass mirror beside the gadget, musical notes arching backwards in neon green and gold, seven tiny glowing app discs orbiting above.
