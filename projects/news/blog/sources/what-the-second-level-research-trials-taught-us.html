What the second-level research trials taught us

What the second-level research trials taught us

The single record of what was learned on 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03 about running the second level of the video direction research cheaply. Tickets, handoffs and the corpus README link here rather than repeating it. Dollar figures on Claude Haiku are notional, from the CLI's reported cost on a subscription.

Terms: Sweep 1 is the first level, 291 discovery harvests. Sweep 2 is the second level, 48,211 planned sub-packets. Round 1 is the first selected batch inside Sweep 2, 2,880 packets, ten per base. A pass is one research attempt on one packet.

Where the tokens went

A worker loop on Haiku cost $0.072 a harvest over 126 harvests, against $0.019 on DeepSeek. Across 11 runs, each harvest averaged about 600,000 cache-read tokens, 56,000 cache-write tokens and 4,850 output tokens, with about 9 Edit calls and 5 searches and fetches. So the money went on re-reading the conversation at each turn, not on writing. Moving search, fetching, validation and file writing into a script, with one model call, removes that loop.

The lesson of version 2: name counts mislead, so read the rows. It beat the baseline on names while giving rows such as "Hat Choke" for a question about rack focus.

The one-call method gives direct answers and says plainly when sources fall short. It trailed the worker loops on rare vocabulary in that reading, and run_oneshot.py runs its searches before seeing any rows, which suggests the two are connected. The two methods overlapped on only 4 to 10 names in 20 packets, so low overlap says little about how much is left to find.

Things that did not work

  • Turning off Haiku's thinking through Claude Code. --effort low, MAX_THINKING_TOKENS=0, alwaysThinkingEnabled: false and CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_THINKING=1 all left empty thinking blocks and about 2,100 to 3,900 output tokens against about 500 visible, on one to three runs each. Asking for summaries returned none. The thinking cannot be read. A provider that lets it be disabled may be much cheaper.
  • Scraped search. Bing's HTML returned Google Drive help for a film query. DuckDuckGo, Mojeek, Yahoo, Startpage and Brave's HTML page returned nothing usable or refused. The Google MCP returned nothing. Keenable, Exa and Parallel, taken from the Hermes agent's web plugin, need no key. Keenable served all searches so far; its limits at volume are unknown.
  • Packet-level @see. Comparing questions finds only 102 near-duplicates at 0.6 similarity, about 0.2% of the queue. At 0.5 and below, groups chain unrelated packets, such as grief with game trailers.

Volume

Running all 48,211 packets is not the aim. The planner wrote that many when told "more is better", and the worker loop would have produced about 450,000 rows nobody could integrate. On Pro, one reading of about 50% of a window suggests about $18 notional a window, which is about 250 worker-loop harvests. Round 1 at about $0.0037 a packet is about $11.

@see links

Brook's design: begin with one entry per idea and fill in detail slowly. Links are alias (the same thing, nothing to fill in) or near (close but different, the fill-in list). Each points directly at one main entry, and harvests are never changed. see_rows.py turns 32% of existing rows into alias-style links for no tokens. see_near.py gave 46 links from 2 of 81 batches for $0.0079. Most looked sensible, and some were doubtful, such as "Broadcast live @see Actual one shot". Its batches are alphabetical within one dimension, so a pair in different batches cannot link, and dolly against zoom may be split.

  • Finds were mixed: pinscreen technique, imaginary focus and snorkel/borescope shots against push-in, Dutch tilts and The Wolf House, a film that should not be a row.

Current judgement, not yet measured

Rich packets should mine saved pages, then chase rare names only while that pays. Unanswered packets should search the question using the model's next_queries and see the earlier answer. Both should be tested against ten more new packets per base, about 100 packets each, comparing new names per dollar after alias matching and then reading a sample. @see fill-in comes last and on demand. See PCB-057 and PCB-058.

Round 1, finished 2026-11-03

All 2,880 packets have a harvest: 11,707 rows, 442 packets with none. Three models did it in relay, 92 packets on Luna, 805 on Terra and 1,983 on Claude Haiku, each switch forced by a usage limit or a decision by Brook. Haiku's share cost $6.76 notional.

What running it taught us about search

  • Free search runs out by the hour. Keenable's public tier allows 1,000 requests an hour per IP; Exa and Parallel returned HTTP 429 at the same time. A free Keenable account removes the hourly cap and allows 100,000 requests a month. With the key, the pace went from stalls to about 130 harvests in five minutes.
  • A cooldown should bench one vendor, not all of them. The first quota handler paused every search for Keenable's whole cooldown (602 s), so all workers idled while Exa and Parallel were free. Skipping only the throttled vendor fixed it.
  • Benching the last vendor stalls everything. Treating Brave's rate-limit replies like the free vendors' benched Brave too, and with every vendor benched the workers slept through a 23-minute cooldown; no worker had a network connection open. The cause was inferred from that, not proved. Brave now paces all processes to one call a second through a lock file and retries its own rate-limit replies.
  • Paid credit runs out too. Brave's prepaid balance returned 402 at about 13:30 and the postpaid account at about 15:35, after 1,440 calls. A 402 now benches Brave for an hour.
  • Retrying an honest empty wastes calls. "No web results found" is an answer, not an error. It is now accepted, and a search that finds nothing is retried in a broader form that the trail states plainly.
  • Detached is not detached enough. Runners started with nohup ended silently when the agent session that launched them closed, inferred from the timing; Sweep 2/detach.py now starts them in their own session.

Redoing empty searches

99 harvests had an empty or failed search. Moved aside and redone once search worked, they gave 248 rows against 167.

Unknown

Quality has been judged by one reader from names and answer openings, with no row checked against its source. The provider route has only run against a local stub. Rate limits at volume, extra-usage billing on the Claude account, and Round 1's final yield are all unmeasured.
