Smart home

Spent most of the afternoon sorting the four Everything Presence Lite/mmWave zone devices — bedroom, dining room, kitchen and living room — into useful signals versus their astonishing pile of configuration and debug clutter. The inventory came to 24 signals worth keeping visible and 303 entities to hide, with the complete lists saved in Documents/hermes/zone_entities_keep_hide.txt and Documents/hermes/zone_hide_lists.json. That cleanup is still only planned: hiding does not disable devices, but the live metadata changes were not applied after the original Home Assistant connection ran into authentication trouble. Kitchen has duplicate occupancy entities; living room exposes raw TEMT6000 voltage rather than a proper illuminance sensor.

Also kept the battery dashboard/low-battery work moving with readable device names: normal warning/critical thresholds are 20%/10%, with higher thresholds for the Acaia scale (40%/15%) and Oral-B toothbrush (30%/15%). Removed one unsuitable sensor from that flow. Tidied Zigbee naming and areas too: the spare control became Button: ambient, and the dining-room control is now Knob: diningroom.

The generic Zigbee2MQTT smart-knob blueprint is now installed as brook/light-knob.yaml, and the bedroom, living-room and dining-room knobs were migrated and reloaded. Double press still swaps the selected light in bedroom and living room; dining room controls its main light directly. Rotation changes brightness and press-and-rotate changes colour temperature. The old command-mode enforcement automation was disabled after its Zigbee write timeouts. Only the physical press/rotation check remains.

In the evening I chased the bedroom/bathroom light oddness, restored the shower night brightness to its original gentle 10%, and finally pinned down the improvement I actually want: occupancy should increase brightness when useful, never pull an already-bright light down. That needs changing the source of brook/light-zone.yaml, not just twiddling an automation input.

The under-bed flame had quietly reached Home Assistant’s 10,000-repeat safety limit after about 1 hour 17 minutes: still on, just no longer flickering. Added an automation to restart the flicker script whenever Flame is enabled, including after a Home Assistant restart, and verified brightness was changing again.
