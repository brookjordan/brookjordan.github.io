Story-song format requirements

PCB-005 · Improve story-song format and lyric presentation

Scope

Revise the song-generation stage in the six-frame PodCast Builder workflow (PodCast Builder - for cursor - up to 6-frame FLF test, S9v0UpCVYcrtVJH4) so the musical segment better serves the episode rather than forcing stories into a rigid short-form lyric template.

Keep the core concept: turn 2–4 of the episode’s top stories into a song. Relax the generation format so the model can use natural phrasing, varied line lengths, verses, refrains, or short narrative passages when that sounds better. Do not require every selected story to receive an identical fixed block of lines.

Requirements

  • Allow songs to run longer than the current roughly 18-second target when that produces a more enjoyable, coherent result.
  • Preserve 2–4 top stories as the source material, but let the generator decide how tightly each story is represented in the lyrics.
  • Remove emoji from lyric output.
  • Format rendered lyrics with real Markdown line breaks and paragraphs, not slash-separated pseudo-lines.
  • Improve the prompt/output contract so structure supports musicality rather than dictating a repetitive, awkward format.
  • Compare song-writing approaches systematically before choosing a production prompt: five representative article sets × five prompt/format ideas × two independent generations each, for 50 listenable songs.
  • Retain any metadata needed downstream for audio generation and episode publication.

Evaluation plan

Build a reviewable test matrix in the six-frame workflow rather than guessing from a single episode:

  • Five article sets: representative mixes of five selected articles, covering different story combinations and tones.
  • Five writing ideas: distinct prompt/format approaches, ranging from lightly structured verse/refrain guidance to more narrative, free-form lyric direction.
  • Two generations per cell: capture variation and avoid promoting an approach based on a lucky one-off output.
  • Total: 5 × 5 × 2 = 50 songs for listening review.

For every generated song, retain the article-set ID, writing-idea ID, generation number, rendered lyrics, audio asset, duration, and any relevant prompt/model metadata. Present the corpus in a form that makes side-by-side listening and lyric comparison practical.

Acceptance checks

  1. Produce the complete 50-song matrix: five article sets × five writing ideas × two generations each.
  2. Every result is available for listening alongside clean lyric text and its recorded test metadata.
  3. Several representative episode runs produce readable lyric text without emoji or slash-delimited lines.
  4. Lyrics can vary in length and structure while still clearly referring to 2–4 selected stories.
  5. The audio duration is not artificially capped at 18 seconds; longer songs are accepted when the result warrants it.
  6. Select a preferred writing approach from the listening review, then validate the modified workflow, inspect connections, and test the six-frame workflow before any production publish.

Notes

This is a content-quality and presentation change. Keep the existing production workflow as rollback until the six-frame test workflow is validated and reviewed.

Progress — 2026-09-16: the intro song had no vocals at all

Episode v2026.38.4-01.01 opened with a wordless intro. The cause was in the lyric channel, not the music model.

YuE's documented input contract is narrow: the lyrics must contain verse and chorus sections and the tags it understands are [verse] and [chorus]. Style and lyrics otherwise reach the model verbatim, with no caption cleaning or lyric normalisation. The generated lyrics had three sections, the first being:

[Intro - Pulsing synth bass and a clean electric guitar hook]

That is a production direction wearing a section tag, so the model performed it — spending the song's opening on an instrumental intro — and the 40-second duration target then expired before the [Verse] arrived. The same class of problem was already recorded for this ticket: intro concepts and production directions leaking into the lyrics channel.

The fix, in Parse song spec:

  • Enforce the contract. The lyric sent to YuE is rebuilt to contain at most two sections, [verse] and [chorus], with inline bracketed and parenthesised stage directions stripped and each section capped at four lines. Unrecognised sections ([Intro], [Bridge], and anything else) are dropped rather than passed through.
  • Remove the duration cap for the sung intro, so the model is never cut off mid-thought. Length is now bounded by the short lyric rather than by a timer, which is what makes the intro usually short without a cap. The instrumental bumper keeps its 12-second target.
  • Drop the now-unused minimax_caption and minimax_lyrics fields, the last MiniMax remnants in the workflow.

Verified by executing the real node code against the real run-2560 data: the intro tag is gone, exactly two canonical sections remain, and no stage directions survive.

Still worth watching: whether a shorter two-section lyric reliably yields a short sung intro in practice, since that depends on the model choosing to sing early. The [Intro] direction is gone, which was the main cause, but this has not yet been confirmed on a fresh generation.
