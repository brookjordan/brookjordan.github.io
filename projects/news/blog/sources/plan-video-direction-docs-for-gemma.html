The plan for video direction docs for Gemma

Plan: video direction docs Gemma can use in the music video

Written 2026-11-03 while Round 1 finished. Owned by the ticket for turning the corpus into Gemma docs. Starting point: the song-style docs built for PCB-047.

Where the docs would be used

The shots stage asks Gemma 4 12B for one direction per video: a frame, a climax, one medium, one to three worlds each with a free-text look, up to three cast, a never list and one beat per act. Each shot then picks framing from three values (wide, medium, close-up) and camera from seven moves (static, slow push in, slow pull out, pan, tilt, tracking, handheld). Medium and look are free text the model invents today.

So the research has four places to land, in order of how much a choice changes the video:

Slot in direction.ts What the corpus offers Corpus dimensions
medium named visual styles, animation techniques, materials, capture media, film movements aesthetic, animation-technique, material, capture-medium; roots B03, C01 to C06
world look colour schemes, light, contrast, period looks, set and texture aesthetic, material, attention; root A03
beats, opening and climax sequence shapes, cutting to music, figurative devices, opening and ending conventions sequence-grammar, shot-function, device; roots A04 to A06, B01
shot framing and camera framings, viewpoints, moves, platforms camera-operation, camera-platform, shot-function; roots A01, A02

This plan leaves sound, attention-research and festival or archive rows in the corpus, because the direction schema asks for nothing they would fill (checked: the required fields and enums in podcastbuilder/src/lib/direction.ts).

How the song-style docs work, and what carries over

Song styles are chosen in two constrained steps. Gemma picks a family from about 29, each a one-line "for X" description, then a style within it from compact cards; each answer is an enum, so it cannot invent a style. The cards were labelled offline by a model panel (Gemma, Qwen 3 8B, Phi-4 mini, then reconciled), assignments live in an editable JSON, and the Markdown family docs are generated from it. Each style entry says use when, suits, feel, signals, against, wrong for and voices.

Carry over: two-level choice, enum answers, a short reason logged with each pick, a generated doc from an editable assignment file, the "against" and "wrong for" fields, and the PCB-047 rule to keep room for bold pairings such as the terrace chant for air strikes.

Why this data needs a different shape

  1. Several choices, not one. A song needs one style; a video needs a medium, a look per world, an opening and climax device, and per-shot moves. So there is one small catalogue per slot, not one catalogue.
  2. The data is raw. Round 1 plus the earlier harvests hold 15,833 rows and 13,078 distinct names, unreviewed. In a 30-row read about 18 were useful, 6 generic and 6 phrases lifted from prose; 3.1% of names were missing from their cited source text. Song styles began as a curated library; these need cleaning first.
  3. Many names for one idea. 2,577 alias and near links and 537 exact-repeat groups exist, across languages (plano próximo, 望遠レンズを多用). An entry must be one idea with its other names folded in, or Gemma sees duplicates as different choices.
  4. Not everything may render. Rack focus, split diopter or a dolly zoom are real direction but may not be controllable in a Qwen-Image still and a five-second LTX or H3 clip, which are also asked for no lettering. A card should say whether the pipeline can show it, and that needs a render test, not a label.
  5. The output is prompt text. A song card feeds a choice; a medium or look card must also give the paragraph pasted into every still prompt, like the mermaid video's ABOVE and BELOW looks, plus what to add to never.

Doc shapes

Medium card, about 60 words for the choice, plus prompt text used only after it is chosen:

  • name, id, also called (from alias links), close variants (from near links, with their difference)
  • use when: what kind of story or song it suits; against: where it fights the story
  • look: one paragraph a still prompt can paste: technique, material, light, palette
  • motion: how movement should read in a clip (stepped frames for stop motion, drifting for watercolour)
  • avoid: what breaks it, added to the direction's never
  • renders: verified, partial or untested, with the test still's path
  • sources: how many harvests name it, and one example work

Look card: the same, without motion, chosen per world.

Device card for openings, climaxes, endings and transitions: purpose, how to stage it in shots of about five seconds, which act it fits, and how many shots it needs.

Shot vocabulary: no cards. Code widens the framing and camera enums only with terms that pass a clip render test, each with the exact wording the clip prompt uses.

Families per slot, built like the song families: for media perhaps photographic periods, painted, drawn, stop motion and materials, collage and cut-out, computer image, experimental, each under about 25 cards so a family listing stays short for Gemma.

Pipeline

Proposed: every step runs from scripts in the corpus repository and writes into podcastbuilder/data/video/, the way the song docs live in podcastbuilder/data/. Under this plan the corpus stays private and Gemma sees only the generated docs.

  1. Freeze Round 1. Finish the run, retry failed packets, run see_near.py once more, and normalise the older Raw tables, some of which put URLs in the confidence column. No tokens beyond the last linking run.
  2. Canonical entries. Fold exact repeats and alias links into one entry per idea; keep near links as variants. Drop rows that fail cheap checks: film titles, names over about six words, names absent from their source. Code only.
  3. Slot labelling by a local panel. Gemma, Qwen and Phi label each entry, in batches of ten as the song index did: which slot, if any; is it something a director can choose; a 40-word card draft from the entry's own rows only. Reconcile disagreements and mark them for review, as style-family-review.json does. Local models, no paid spend.
  4. Families and cards. Group each slot's entries into families with an editable assignment file, generate the Markdown docs and a JSON index from it, and have Brook read a sample of each family before anything ships.
  5. Render qualification. One still per medium and look card, and one clip per proposed camera move, judged by the existing stills judge. Cards below the bar stay untested or partial and are not offered. Research the image and video models' known limits first, then test only an approved shortlist.
  6. Wire into direction.ts. Before writing the direction, Gemma picks a medium family, then a medium card, each as an enum with a logged reason; the card's look and avoid text are pasted in rather than invented. Then a look per world, then an opening and climax device. Code builds each shortlist of five to eight cards, and one shortlist slot is drawn at random so a bold pairing stays possible.
  7. Evaluate. Against today's free-text direction on the same songs: stills judge pass rate (PCB-050's bar is 80% on first planning), and Brook's preference on at least three episodes, as PCB-047 set for song styles.

Gaps found in steps 4 and 5, such as a family with few renderable cards, become the packet selection for Round 2 instead of more of the same.

Open decisions for Brook

  • Which slot first. Recommendation: medium, then world look, because they change every shot and mirror the song-style choice most closely.
  • How much review per family before it ships: a sample read, or every card.
  • Whether render qualification may use the GPU between podcast runs.

Not decided here

The exact families, card word limits and shortlist size are to be set from the step 3 output, not in advance.
