The spark

Before any of the gallery machinery, there was a much smaller goal: an n8n workflow (grandly named "PodCast Builder") to auto-generate hero images for a patch-notes / podcast blog. The n8n logs show it grinding away from 09:57 on the 22nd — including one heroic 1h30m run.

And it wasn't only covers — Patch Notes is a podcast, so the day's very first AI experiments were a voice: five AI-generated intro takes (ACE-Step audio; intro_302 → intro_317) between 10:37 and 14:57 that morning, hours before a single cover image existed. Here's take 305 (Jun 22, 11:28).

"trying to build an n8n process for my blog and realising the images weren't as good quality / in the right style as I wanted, and wondering how to get more out of local image generation."

The first frames landed late that night — 23:33 on the 22nd — and they were… a lot. Melting golden clocks, robot ants swarming a circuit-board canyon, skies full of binary. On-theme for "patch notes" (time! bugs! code!) and genuinely striking — but a touch generic-AI, over-saturated, and nowhere near consistent enough to be a blog's recurring look.

flux4b-fast · 23:33 flux9b-hero · 23:36 chroma-art · 23:54

The PN_ prefix is "Patch Notes" — the podcast's working title — and the -fast / -hero / -art suffixes were already me groping for a style — a fast daily look, a polished hero look, an arty look.

And that's the exact moment this project was really born. Not "make one image," but the nagging question:

Which local model, at what step count, actually gives the look I want — and how do I get the most out of this Mac?

You can't answer that by eyeballing one render. You answer it by rendering everything — every model, every step count, the same scenes, side by side. Which is precisely what we did over the next three days. Welcome to the rabbit hole.
