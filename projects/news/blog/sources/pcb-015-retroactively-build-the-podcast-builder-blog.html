PCB-015 brief

PCB-015 Retroactively build the podcast builder blog

Question

What does the Podcast Builder's history look like when told as a story — and can a deep dive through the vault's evidence produce the first posts of a builder blog in the same spirit as the local image gen project's blog?

Brief

A weaker agent is deliberately the right tool here: the job is reading vault history, not writing prose. It should go back in time through the evidence listed below, reconstruct what actually happened, and draft blog posts from it.

Voice and tone (this is the point of the ticket): tongue-in-cheek, fun to read, first-person-plural confessional. NOT a white paper. Think "we taught a computer to kill the intro silence and it deleted the whole chorus" energy, not "Phase 2 optimised the TTS latency envelope". The local image gen project blog is the house style — match its register, structure and self-deprecation. Jokes should land because they are true.

Evidence trail (read in this order)

  1. Map + tickets — the podcast builder map and PCB-001 through PCB-015; every ticket has a story arc (decision, failure, fix).
  2. Daily diary beats — Daily/2026-08-17 onward; the map's own "Recent diary" section is a curated index.
  3. Scratchpad packets — the podcast scratchpad index, especially the 2026-09-15 NAS/ComfyUI preflight conversation capture.
  4. The failure museum — SIGBUS death when the NAS vanished mid-run (run 2567); the ComfyUI lock collision (execution 2415); the archived-but-critical workflow node (Plan verified animation endpoints) that broke both builders; the MiniMax lyric that sang "Intro song" as literal lyrics; Delete MP3s via SSH throwing after every successful publish; the 2026-09-19 server kill that took a render down with the production schedule's profile swap.
  5. The LTX envelope project — the LTX-2.5 operating-envelope map for the overnight-run sagas, the duration boundary, and the 2026-09-19 uncapped-watermark adventure.
  6. Today's 10-second arc — PCB-013 and the probe packet: binary-searching a memory ceiling, negotiating with an OOM, and the smoke that flew up then came back down.

Deliverables

  • 3–5 draft blog posts covering: the YuE2/LTX cutover (PCB-006), the SIGBUS incident and the local-models fix (PCB-008), the failure-alert saga, and the 10-second duration probes.
  • Each post grounded in at least two linked evidence notes; no invented timestamps or quotes.
  • Drafts reviewed by Brook before publishing; publishing destination decided separately.
