PodCast Builder · devlog

Written retrospectively from records covering 15–19 September 2026.

We wanted a clean answer to an awkwardly specific question: could one of the frozen podcast cases make a ten-second transition? Case C was the overgrown mythical-ruins scene, source execution 2572, with a locked camera and slow fog drifting towards the lens. The recorded result was not a triumphal “LTX solved video”. It was one measured run under one changed memory condition, which is less cinematic and considerably more useful.

What happened

The probe used case C's verified cover pair and the motion plan recorded for execution 2572. The target was 250 frames at 25 fps, recorded as 9.96 seconds in the evidence packet. Under the normal 0.8 MPS watermark, the 250-frame render failed twice at roughly six minutes in: the record says 20.71 GiB held and another 1.83 GiB refused. The failure was repeated rather than treated as bad luck. The probe then ran with the MPS watermark disabled and completed in 2,196 seconds, with endpoint measurements recorded as 4.2 and 3.3 out of 255.

What the evidence changed

The distinction between requested and decoded frames stayed visible. The case notes describe the target as 250 requested frames, while the completed probe is recorded as 249 decoded frames and 9.96 seconds. We did not silently turn those into the same field. A request is what the graph asks for; decoded output is what the media evidence reports. Both belong in the ledger.

The memory result also changed the question. The default cap was the immediate boundary, not proof that the model could never produce the clip. Removing that safeguard for the probe allowed the render to finish, but it was a changed operating condition, not a recipe to recommend for production. The packet reports a roughly linear wall-time trend above the load phase, and separately records a tiled VAE decode event at the end. One completed case could show feasibility under that condition; it could not certify safety, quality, or generality.

Where we left it

Case C therefore earned a limited milestone: a recorded 9.96-second decoded clip under the uncapped server condition, delivered as /tmp/pcb012-caseC-10s.mp4 in the ticket evidence. The result did not close the five-case probe. Cases A, B, D, and E remained queued in the captured state, and the ticket still listed five attempts, per-case memory, endpoint fidelity, and visual verdict as open acceptance work.

We had a render that made it through. We had not earned permission to move the production limit, and the memory safeguard was not a magic spell to copy into a scheduled workflow. The sensible next step was boring accounting: finish or record the other cases, preserve their conditions, and review the media rather than turning one green line into a policy.
