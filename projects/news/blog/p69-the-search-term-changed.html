The search term changed, and now it tells you

A search that finds nothing now broadens itself, and says so in plain words: what changed and why. Redoing 99 harvests that had empty searches gave 248 rows against 167, though the ladder itself has not been needed yet.

'Can we have the agent attempt a modified search term in these situations?' Brook asked, about the searches that came back with nothing. And then, before a line of code existed, a second rule: the agent must say plainly that it changed the search term, and what the change was. Two messages, and a whole feature with its own honesty policy!

How empty is empty?

We counted first. Across Round 1, 99 harvests had at least one search that came back empty or failed, 241 such searches in all. Most were honest empties: a search for 'maracas bachata fotograma' simply has very few pages behind it. Twenty-four were refusals, when every engine was cooling down or out of credit.

The broadening ladder

A search that finds nothing now climbs a short ladder before it gives up:

  1. The same words, without quotation marks.
  2. Only its four most distinctive words, with filler such as 'film terminology glossary also known as' dropped.
  3. If the whole packet still has no usable pages, one model call writes three broader searches, in English, a local language or trade terms, and the packet searches again.

Every rung is free except the last, and the last fires only when nothing else worked.

Saying so, out loud, three times

Brook's second rule is the good bit. A harvest that quietly searched for something other than its question would look exactly like one that did not. So every change is written down where a reader will trip over it:

Changed search term: `"maracas bachata fotograma"` found nothing, so searched
`maracas bachata fotograma` instead (removed the quotation marks); via keenable: 8 results.

That line goes in the trail. A notice under the harvest's title says how many times its terms were changed. And the cost log gets a search_changes count, so changed harvests can be found without reading 2,880 files. Two tests check both the change and its wording.

The redo

Then Brook asked for the 99 to be redone. Each old harvest and its saved pages moved into a 'superseded' folder with a manifest of hashes, so nothing was deleted, and a fifth runner took the list. All 99 came back with no empty search at all, and with 248 rows between them against the old 167. NEARLY HALF AGAIN AS MANY.

Rejected, without ceremony

  • Change the search silently. The harvest would then answer a question nobody asked, and nobody could tell. Next!
  • Let the model rewrite every search, every time. A model call per search to fix a problem most searches never have. Next!

What still hurts

The ladder has a perfect record: it has never been used. By the time the redo ran, the free Keenable account key was in, and every one of those 99 packets found results on its first try. So the extra rows came from search working again, not from the ladder, and the ladder is tested only by its own unit tests. Its first real outing is still ahead of it, and we will be insufferable about it when it comes.
