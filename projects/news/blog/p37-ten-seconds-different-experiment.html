PodCast Builder · devlog

Written retrospectively from records covering 15–19 September 2026.

The inherited longer-video expectation was sensible, and still insufficient. The operating-envelope notes described a clean single-render result around 20 seconds and a practical ceiling near 24 seconds. Our podcast question was different: whether real episode prompts and their paired images could survive a longer transition in the graph we intended to use. Same family of model, different recipe, different evidence.

What happened

The earlier envelope work used a two-stage recipe with Q5 quantisation at its centre. The podcast probe was deliberately narrower and more production-shaped: one-stage Q3_K_M distilled inference, 1280×704 latent resolution, first/last-frame conditioning, a manual nine-sigma schedule, tiled VAE decoding, and 25 fps. The distinction matters because a recipe is not a decorative list of knobs. It determines memory allocation, timing, and how the model treats the guide frames. The probe packet records a 49-frame control at 1.96 seconds and a 121-frame run at 4.84 seconds before the longer attempt.

What the evidence changed

We stopped treating duration as a transferable property of “LTX” in the abstract. The two-stage/Q5 result in the longer-video ticket remained useful evidence about that configuration; it was not invalidated by the one-stage/Q3_K_M probe. It simply answered a different question. The probe's capped 250-frame attempt requested 9.96 seconds and failed twice at the MPS watermark. The same frame count succeeded when the server was launched uncapped, but that changed condition had to remain explicit. We could compare within a recipe, not borrow a ceiling across recipes.

This is the unglamorous architecture lesson: the unit under test is model, quantisation, stage count, resolution, conditioning, scheduler, hardware path, and memory policy together. We wanted a duration number. The records handed us a configuration boundary instead.

Where we left it

The five-case podcast probe therefore asks whether each frozen real case can produce a 10-second clip under the recorded one-stage production transition graph, not whether the entire LTX setup can safely make 20-second videos. Case C produced a recorded 9.96-second result under the uncapped condition; cases A, B, D, and E were still queued or pending in the captured state. The longer 15-render PCB-012 matrix remained separate and incomplete.

We had demonstrated that evidence does not transfer unchanged merely because the headline model name matches. We had not established a general duration limit, a production-safe memory policy, or completion of the five-case set. That restraint was the useful result: the next experiment could name its controls instead of inheriting a number by folklore. A duration claim without its recipe is a measurement with its labels torn off. That is why the post keeps the inconvenient details in view, even though they make the headline less exciting. They are the difference between a useful boundary and a campfire story about a model that “can do twenty seconds”.
