Freeze the test cases before you touch the duration knob

A duration experiment is very good at producing a number that belongs to a different subject. Five real podcast cases were locked, hash-verified and documented before the limit moved an inch.

Written retrospectively from records covering 15–19 September 2026.

A duration experiment is very good at producing a number that belongs to a different subject. We avoided that trap by freezing five real podcast cases before changing the limit. The point was not to assemble five attractive stills. It was to preserve the episode material, prompt lineage, and source-image identities that the later video claim would depend on.

What happened

The frozen packet records five source executions and their motion categories: atmospheric drift in case A, mercury surface motion in B, a fog reveal in C, mechanical breathing in D, and a localised hologram pulse in E. Each case kept its recorded image prompt, motion plan, and cover pair. The experiment's intended accounting was explicit: three LTX renders per case, fifteen renders total, producing five single-prompt videos and five joined two-part videos when complete.

The packet also records why these were useful rather than interchangeable. Some cases were green controls, while others deliberately carried fluid, fog, mechanical, or emissive risks. That variation mattered because a duration result from a locked studio scene would not answer what happened to a breathing subject or a mercury ripple.

What the evidence changed

Freezing the inputs made identity a gate before quality. The provenance note says all ten source files were verified on the NAS and that local and NAS hashes matched. Case E required particular care: execution 2506 was a retry of 2503, and the published cover.png and cover-alt.png pair was the actual frozen source. The original execution-named files did not exist in the expected form. The packet preserves that correction instead of making the filename look tidier.

Hash verification established that the source bytes were the intended bytes. It did not establish that any video rendered well, that a prompt was followed, or that a duration was safe. Those are later measurements. The decision was therefore to verify source identity before varying duration, not to substitute an easier case when a path became inconvenient.

Where we left it

The five cases were frozen and cleared for the planned experiment. The recorded state still had no renders in the freeze packet itself, and the subsequent 10-second probe had only case C completed in the captured horizon. Cases A, B, D, and E remained pending there. The fifteen-render accounting was therefore a plan with a provenance gate, not a completed test matrix.

We moved the limit only after fixing what counted as the same input. It is a small piece of bureaucracy, but without it a duration result can quietly become a result about whichever image happened to be available. Freezing also made the later comparison auditable: when a case failed, the failure could belong to the recipe or memory boundary rather than an accidental source swap.
