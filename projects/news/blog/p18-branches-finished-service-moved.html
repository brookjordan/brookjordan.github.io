Two workflow branches completed flawlessly, the join waited for both, and the final download met ECONNREFUSED on port 8188. The service had changed ports between the readiness check and the download. Timing is a dependency too.

Execution 2503 looked like the sort of run that should have been boring. The normal branch finished its work, the music branch finished its work, and the join waited for both. Then the final download met ECONNREFUSED on port 8188. We had successfully completed two branches and still failed to have the service we needed. The graph was technically synchronised and operationally confused.

What happened

The recorded sequence shows the video branch restoring normal ComfyUI at 08:48:00. The parallel YuE2 branch switched to the music service at 08:48:20. The join then allowed Finalise podcast media to continue, but the old graph went directly to publication preparation. By the time Download final media ran, port 8188 was no longer serving the normal profile. A join in n8n only proves that upstream nodes supplied data. It does not prove that an external service is still in the state the next node expects.

This was a profile race, not evidence that either branch had failed to finish its own work. The timestamps are retained as recorded; the source uses the workflow's Asia/Singapore timezone, so we do not convert them into a different zone for dramatic effect.

What the evidence changed

The correction was a post-merge barrier. The duplicate now routes Finalise podcast media through Restore normal ComfyUI before final media and Assert normal ComfyUI before final media. The barrier uses the same execution lock and checks that port 8188, VHS_LoadVideoPath and VHS_VideoCombine are available before requesting final media. In ordinary language, we stopped trusting the branches to leave the shared service in the right mode and checked the mode at the point where it mattered.

Side-effect-free execution 2505 started with music on 8189 and nothing on 8188. The new barrier started normal ComfyUI, passed its assertion, and downloaded and normalised all 30 binaries totalling 9,329,116 bytes. That validates the barrier's boundary against the recorded artefacts. It does not turn every future run into a guarantee, nor does it make a join a service-health check by magic.

Where we left it

The old graph had a plausible shape and an unsafe assumption: branch completion implied shared-service readiness. The corrected graph made readiness explicit at the merge boundary. The validation covered the barrier, the ports and the final-media read against real stored artefacts. The record does not establish that the full production workflow was thereafter immune to unrelated failures.

Our lesson was pleasantly unglamorous: when two branches share a mutable service, the last node before the shared service must assert its state. Otherwise "both branches finished" is merely a sentence with excellent timing.

The check belonged exactly where the shared dependency resumed, which is where our confidence had previously wandered off.
