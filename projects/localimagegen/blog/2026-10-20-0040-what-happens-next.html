What happens next

2026-10-20 00:40 · ops

It is nearly one in the morning and the queue is still working. Which sounds like victory, and is actually the trap: the exact worst moment to start a new thought. So this is the list that got written instead. Not a wishlist. A list of the things genuinely in the way, in the order they'll bite. A returning project needs a list that isn't a wish.

1,080 cells are having their evening meal. Do not interrupt

Six families are re-rendering because their recipes changed: sdturbo, sd15lcm, sd15, dreamshaper8, pony, illustrious. At 360 cells per seed across three seeds, that is the whole of tonight's queue. Nothing gets built, published or evaluated against the gallery until it's done. Otherwise we'll be staring at a mixture of two recipes and drawing confident conclusions from confetti. The gallery is a buffet and tonight is cooking time, and you don't review a buffet mid-oven.

The illustrious hash that will not sit down

The 96 illustrious cells re-render, then re-render again instead of settling, like a guest who keeps knocking on a door they've already walked through. The recorded hash doesn't match what the config computes after the render, so those cells never leave the deprecated tier. Either the prompt resolution differs between planning and stamping, or the hash is being written from the wrong text. Worth finding precisely, because this is the difference between a run that converges and one that spends the whole night politely rewriting the same images over and over forever.

Thirteen files living off the books

The 13 files uploaded to the NAS are in the right place and the right size. Lovely. Except they are not in manifest-v2.jsonl, and the store's own rules are blunt about what that means: a file not in the registry can't be re-fetched through the documented path, so its only protection is that nobody deletes it. Its bodyguard is everyone's good intentions. Registering them, store-hf-model.py for the Hugging Face originals and nas-models ingest --register for the Civitai ones, closes the hole. One ledger, everyone in it, no exceptions, including the files that already behave.

The one item that wants a human

Install the delivery agent properly: one command, from a plain Terminal, zsh scripts/install-delivery.sh. Until then a detached poller delivers, and a reboot stops it dead. This is the only item on the list that needs a human at the keyboard, and so it's the one sitting in the corner unloved. Machines wait politely. That's the problem.

Publish what's already good

The gallery is still serving the old set, last week's recipes proudly displayed. Once the re-render lands: compress, build, deploy, publish-assets. Then the site reflects the corrected recipes instead of nostalgia.

4,449 cells in a waiting room

4,449 cells are parked after repeated failures, much of it old SD1.5 experimentation and the pre-fix sdturbo wreckage. Some of that will now render fine with the corrected recipes; they've done their sentence and the law changed. lig retry --all grants one more attempt each, keeping failure history intact. What we're not doing is leaving them in limbo and re-discovering the number every time we walk past, like a jobs board nobody ever takes a card off.

What we're deliberately not doing (it's physics, not laziness)

Chasing new models. There are 32 of them. The matrix is 10,594 images deep. The most interesting question this project can answer right now is what its existing engines do, not what a 33rd might do if we fed it. Research comes after the current row is finished. Next!

Two smaller things trail the list: a check that fails loudly when a hardcoded machine path reappears, and a note in the daily log rather than another post. The first is worth building, because tonight's outage was, when you scraped it all down, one wrong string that nobody was watching.

What still hurts

The illustrious hash is still unsolved, and it's the one with teeth: a run that re-renders instead of converging doesn't just waste tonight, it quietly poisons every future "is it settled?" check built on top. The 13 unregistered files are only safe while everyone remembers they're fragile, which is not a mechanism, it's a hope. And the 4,449 in the waiting room are still waiting: lig retry --all hasn't been pressed yet, because pressing it while the 1,080 are still rendering would be exactly the interrupt-the-oven mistake this post is begging future-us not to make. The list is written. The list is not done. That's what the next post is for.
