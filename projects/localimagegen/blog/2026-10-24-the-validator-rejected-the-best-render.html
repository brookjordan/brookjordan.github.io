The validator rejected the best render in the room

2026-10-24 16:30 · incident lesson verify

A MOSS cell failed sixty-three times in one night. Every single failure was the same message:

rejected mossbase_instructions_s25_00031_.png: flat-fill (93% one grey); raw output preserved

The obvious reading is that MOSS Base was broken. Sixty-three failures! That model must be soup! That reading is wrong, and the actual culprit turns out to be the one component whose whole job is being right.

The render was fine

I opened the rejected file, braced for garbage, and found three glazed cat figurines stacked in the order the prompt asked for: cobalt blue at the bottom, mustard yellow in the middle, deep crimson on top. A lemon balanced on the topmost cat's head. The cloth repair patch stitched onto the middle cat exactly where the scene brief puts it, whip-stitches biting into the glaze.

That is a correct render of a correct prompt. The model did the job, did it well, and got arrested for it.

What the gate measured

The check converted the image to greyscale, found the most common grey, and counted the pixels within ±2 levels of it. If that share exceeded 92%, the frame was declared a placeholder, the shape of Ideogram's "Image blocked by safety filter" card.

Here is the arithmetic for the rejected file:
Total pixels 921,600
Modal grey 222
Pixels within ±2 855,449
Share 92.82%
Threshold 92%

It missed by 0.82 percentage points, about 7,500 pixels. And it was going to miss by exactly that much every single time, because the scene asks for "a plain seamless pale-grey studio background with soft, even, shadowless lighting". A correct render of that prompt is supposed to be almost entirely one grey! The deterministic failure was the scene being on brief. The gate arrested the best render in the room for looking exactly like what it asked for.

Why the threshold was wrong

In the gate's defence, the 92% was calibrated honestly. Across 2,150+ committed renders the highest legitimate value was 86.3%, and a refusal card sits at 98%. Nine-two was six points of headroom in both directions. Sensible! Impregnable, even!

But the calibration corpus contained no studio product shots. The gate had never seen a valid image that was mostly background, so it learned 86.3% as the ceiling of normality and drew the line just above it. The comment in src/verify.ts even warned about this: "If a future model produces legitimately near-monochrome mid-grey output, this will flag it." It did. The prophecy came true to the decimal point.

The fix asks a better question

"Is the frame mostly one grey?" cannot separate a refusal card from a studio shot. Both are mostly one colour. The question that can is: did anything render, and is there enough variety to matter?

The replacement measures, in colour, the share of pixels that differ from the dominant colour by more than ~8% per channel:

Share differing Verdict
< 0.01% failed, nothing rendered. File kept.
0.01 to 8% accepted and marked monotonous. Published and delivered.
≥ 8% normal

Measured across 600 real masters: median 50.9% differing, p5 1.8%. Every frame under the 0.01% floor is a genuine blank, the anima two-step rungs are 772-byte pure-black files. The floor still does its job; the 8% line is a note, not a verdict.

The loop was the symptom

Because the rejection was deterministic, the run treated the cell as unfinished and re-rendered it. Same seed, same graph, same output, byte-identical, all 65 s10 files share one MD5. Sixty-three attempts, roughly 1.5 hours of GPU, on a cell that had succeeded on the first try. The run worked like a hammer looking for a nail it had already flattened.

What still hurts

Two lessons, and the second is the one worth keeping:

  1. A validation gate is a hypothesis about what failure looks like. Its corpus is part of its definition. A gate calibrated only on scenes you already render is a gate that will reject the first new kind of scene you try. It is not being mean. It genuinely has never seen anything else.
  2. When a failure is deterministic, retrying is never the answer. Fixing the retry cap would have hidden this bug behind a tidier failure, 63 good renders buried instead of 63 rejected. The loop is what made it visible; it was never the root.

The banner is a small joke at its own expense: ComfyUI produced the still-life happily but kept dropping the red seal, so the seal is drawn with SVG instead. A model that renders an image correctly and ignores the instruction laid over it is the same story in miniature.
