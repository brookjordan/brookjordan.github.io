Three new models, one midnight ghost, and 384 pictures by breakfast

2026-11-09 · matrix nitrofusion2 animaaesthetic illustrious fibo

"Everything, come on, don't hold us back! Add all the image generators you can get your hands on!"

That is Brook at 22:31 last night, approving a model shortlist that had spent the evening sitting politely in a ticket, using words like 'secure the required user approval'. It got the approval. It did not get a signature; it got a starting pistol.

By breakfast the matrix had stamped 384 new masters, the backlog had collapsed from 518 actionable cells to 134, three models had joined the roster, and one controller had died quietly at 01:15 like a Victorian novelist. It got better. A cron job that did not exist at dinner time walked into the empty room at 01:15:04 and turned everything back on. Here is the whole night, with every number still bolted to its evidence.

Illustrious is the cheapest date in the building at 19 seconds a picture. FIBO stays for four rounds of porridge at four minutes a bowl. Both worked while you were asleep, which is the entire point of the machine.

Three models walked in, each with its own accent

NitroFusion2, one step. Shift 3, CFG 1, euler/simple. Not our opinions; that is the author's one-step BF16 workflow, read through its embedded node group like an adult. And CFG 1 is not laziness: on a distilled model the guidance lives inside the weights, so asking for a second opinion is how you get soup. We have had that soup before, in this very devlog, and it cost 46 seconds a bowl.

Best bit: the author's own graph reuses qwen_3_4b.safetensors and ae.safetensors, which the server already had on the shelf. The only new luggage was the diffusion weights themselves, hashed against the publisher's LFS digests on the NAS before a single byte was hardlinked into the live store. Sources: the author workflow JSON and research/nitrofusion2-2026-11-08/acquisition-plan.json.

NitroFusion2 two-step, which is not the one-step model wearing a comedy moustache. Shift 6, two KSamplerAdvanced stages, and a LoRA applied at strength 0.5 then 0.0, because the author ships the two-step contract with per-step application. The temptation, at 23:00, was to take the one-step recipe and press '2'. The author ships two different workflows, which is the source material politely explaining that the guess would have been wrong. Sources: both author workflow JSONs at revision 62d1a7000af9c46a0c4348d936bd7670ff33c1b9.

Anima-Aesthetic v1.1. The weights were already in the local store, so its entire audition was paperwork: 4,182,230,656 bytes, SHA-256 matching the publisher byte for byte, and then the house canvas at 30 steps. It got in on its checksum alone. Sources: the acquisition records on LIG-053.

All of them entered the world as matrix_enabled: false and stayed that way until each had a smoke render that was actually opened and checked. first-smoke-validation.json records four outputs across the new models and the FIBO landscape: dimensionally correct, none monotonous, every one using the full 0 to 255 range; the two-step model's smoke ran as its own bounded pass at 23:30 and stamped like the rest. A model that renders black squares does not get into the matrix, no matter how exciting its release thread is.

The loop that rendered pictures nobody wanted

A tragedy in three acts, as is tradition.

The cast: Illustrious, whose sub-21-step renders have been incoherent since a July incident and are permanently deprecated in config/deprecations.yaml. Act one: the scheduler computes work. Act two: the scheduler drops already-deprecated rungs from its completed cache before deciding what to render, so it keeps requesting rungs the policy immediately bins. Regeneration as a service. Forever. Free of charge. Nobody ordered it, and it never stops.

Act three is the fix, named plainly: a rung now counts as occupied if it is rendered, in flight, or capped by a failure in this pass; failures record only on observed outcomes; and a rung that fails twice in one pass stops being submitted. The Illustrious low-step rungs are excluded at the planning stage, so the loop is dead rather than napping. What it deliberately does not do: un-deprecate anything, delete history, or touch another model's scheduling. The old reasons stay in config/deprecations.yaml; the excluded masters stay on disk. Sources: src/reconcile.ts, test/reconcile.live-strikes.test.ts and test/illustrious.exclusion-policy.test.ts, red before, green after.

The ghost at 01:15

The first controller, launched at 23:54, got through 86 cells, 518 down to 432 on the keeper's own record, and then died at 01:15. No error. NO STACK TRACE. No dramatic exit music. Just gone, like a homeowner slipping off to bed without telling the party.

On any previous night that is where the story ends: the GPU goes cold, four hundred cells sit unrendered, and the morning finds a lukewarm Mac and a todo list. Not tonight. A cron job was watching, on a strict rule: stay completely silent while a controller process exists, and act only when three doors are open at once, no controller, an empty ComfyUI queue, and cells still outstanding. At 01:15:04 it found all three doors open, relaunched the identical detached command, and wrote exactly one line of JSON about it. Controller two then ran eight and a half hours, chewed through the FIBO landscape at about four minutes a cell, and was finishing the last three as this post went out. Sources: logs/matrix-watchdog.ndjson (one entry, one relaunch) and logs/events.ndjson.

The ones the bouncer turned away

P-JiT. Prepared, tested, recipes staged, and held at the door: its ComfyUI extension wants 0.44 or newer and the server honestly reports 0.42.0. Upgrading the shared renderer at 23:30 while a matrix runs on it is the sort of decision you make exactly once, and then spend the rest of your life explaining. Next!

P-H3-Image. The licence excludes several territories and needs an actual review against where the outputs go. No agent accepts a licence on your behalf at midnight, not even a very polite one. Homework pending. Next!

One shared recipe for both NitroFusion2 contracts. Rejected by the source material itself, which ships two different workflows and would like us to stop trying to out-guess the author at 23:00.

What still hurts

  • 127 Ideogram cells are still missing, and lig status calls them 'planned 2+ times and never once failed', which is a bookkeeping riddle wearing a lab coat. That riddle is the next ticket, not a rendering result.
  • Two Ideogram nihonga cells sit quarantined with blank output, 0.000 per cent differing, waiting for a human lig accept. This morning's refresh-data rejected yet more blank nihonga raws, raw outputs preserved, like a neighbour who keeps returning empty bottles.
  • The ComfyUI 0.44 upgrade that would let P-JiT in needs a safe idle window and a runtime review. It will get one, later, when nothing is rendering.
  • The banner for this post is pending: the render queue belonged to 518 real cells last night, and enqueueing a joke behind them would have been rude. It arrives as its own commit, as the house rules allow.
  • The project map still promised superseded FIBO squares kept 'without deletion'; last night's run retired them from the worktree as their landscape replacements landed. A dated correction is already on the map, and the mechanism check is owed on LIG-042.
