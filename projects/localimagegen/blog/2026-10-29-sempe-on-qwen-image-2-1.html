Sempé on Qwen Image 2.1, or how to draw a very small man in a very large town

2026-10-29 · lesson content

"None of the styles look anything like they should… try other models."

That was Brook, looking at three test frames for a music video about Harrow, Bracknell and Hyères, the one he wants to show his sister in Hyères. Each town was meant to get its own French illustrator. Harrow got Jean-Jacques Sempé, of Le Petit Nicolas and a hundred-odd New Yorker covers, the man who draws tiny people under enormous skies. What Harrow actually got was a very competent drawing by nobody in particular.

The polite prompt

The first try ran on Qwen Image 2512, the pipeline's stills model, with the scene first and the style after it:

A concrete pedestrian footbridge over an empty ring road at dusk in the rain, one small figure with an umbrella crossing it, ... Drawn in fine pen and Indian ink with loose, transparent watercolour washes on white paper, in the manner of Jean-Jacques Sempe: seen from a high, distant viewpoint so the figure is tiny in a wide, detailed townscape, much untouched white paper, gentle and wistful, washes of sodium orange and slate grey.

It is ink, and it is watercolour. It is also detail from edge to edge, with the white paper buried alive, and white paper is the one thing Sempé always leaves. This is a drawing of someone who once heard Sempé described at a party.

Say his name, then describe him until it hurts

Brook's rule for round two was to name the illustrator and describe the style heavily, because these may not be artists the models ever trained on. So the style leads the prompt, runs to a full paragraph, and comes back after the scene:

An illustration by Jean-Jacques Sempé, the French cartoonist of Le Petit Nicolas and of New Yorker covers. Drawn with a fine, scratchy, nervous dip-pen line in black Indian ink, the lines thin, broken and loosely hatched; pale, loose, transparent watercolour washes laid over the ink and bleeding past the lines; large areas of bare white paper left untouched; an enormous scene seen from high up and far away, the people tiny, almost dots, dwarfed by buildings and sky; light, airy, gentle and wistful; soft washes of sodium orange and slate grey. [Alexandra Avenue in Harrow, a motorcycle shop, two tiny passers-by.] The whole image is a hand-drawn ink and watercolour cartoon on white paper in Sempé's manner, with tiny figures and wide white space. Not a photograph, not a digital painting, not a 3D render.

Same seed, 424242. Same scene. Three models.

Qwen Image 2.1 gets the paper back

THE WHITE PAPER IS BACK. There's a nervous, broken pen line, washes that wander past their outlines, and two passers-by small enough to lose. Brook picked it on sight. It took 94 seconds at 30 steps on the official template recipe: qwen_image_2.1_int8_convrot, Euler and simple, CFG 1.

The other two, for the record:

Krea 2 went picture-book, which is charming and the wrong kind of whimsy. SDXL drew a lovely Haussmann boulevard in fine ink, as if Harrow had quietly applied for a French passport.

And then it nearly didn't happen

The video's first real still came back like this:

Ink and watercolour, sure. Also gloomy, close-up and bruised purple, about as Sempé as a power cut. The model wasn't the problem. The pipeline's shots stage compiles every still's prompt once, into shots/shots.json, before anyone edits the direction. So the hand-written Sempé lead sat in the direction file looking pleased with itself and never reached a prompt. The model was sent the stage's own text: "Charcoal and watercolor animation on textured paper... a grimy, subterranean basement hallway... the palette is industrial grey and bruised purple." The switch to Qwen Image 2.1 did apply, because the stage reads the model live, so this is Qwen 2.1 drawing the wrong prompt very faithfully.

We caught it at shot 1 of 40, so it cost two renders. The fix was a recompile, with scripts/recompile-stills.ts in podcastbuilder rebuilding every prompt from the edited direction, and a rewrite of the world's look so its light and palette agree with Sempé. Here is the same shot, model and seed, with the lead actually first:

THERE he is. A scratchy line, washes that wander, white paper at the edges, and a small man in a big wet street. Brook called it "a total win". The lesson is boring, so it's worth writing down: check the prompt that was sent, not the one you wrote.

All nine frames

Rows are Sempé, Tardi and Cassandre. Columns are Qwen Image 2.1, Krea 2 Turbo, and SDXL base with the 8-step Lightning LoRA.

Nobody expected this, Brook included. No model won all three. Qwen 2.1 took Sempé, SDXL took Tardi, Krea 2 took Cassandre. The Tardi post and the Cassandre post have the other two rows.

What still hurts

  • Three things changed between the polite try and the winner: the prompt order, the prompt's weight, and the model, from 2512 to 2.1. This post can't say which one did the work. Running 2512 with the heavy prompt would.
  • One seed, one judge, one scene. It's a ranking, not a measurement.
  • Nobody has shown that Qwen knows the name Sempé. The description may be doing all of it.
