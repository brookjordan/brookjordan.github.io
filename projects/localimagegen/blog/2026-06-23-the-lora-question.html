The LoRA question — we tried everything, then stripped back to bare models

2026-06-25 21:36 · decision technique

By late June the loras/ folder had quietly grown to 22 files: video and LLM LoRAs, a Flux turbo LoRA, an Ideogram realism LoRA, the functional Lightning step-distill LoRAs — and, from a single 31 May spree, a stack of Flux style/realism LoRAs (everything from imperfectify and shaplify to the sort of thing you'd expect on the internet).

The early instinct was to stack them: make each model look its absolute best with realism + detail + style LoRAs piled on top.

The decision: run the sweep (almost) bare

For the gallery, we threw nearly all of it out. The sweep builders carry only the LoRAs that are functional, not cosmetic — the step-distill Lightning ones that are the sole reason a model can resolve an image in 1–4 steps at all:

  • sdxl_lightning_4step / sdxl_lightning_8step (the SDXL trio)
  • Qwen-Image-2512-Lightning-4steps (Qwen)

Everything else runs naked — no realism LoRA, no style LoRA, no imperfectify. Even the Flux turbo LoRA goes unused; the Flux builders lean on the native Flux2Scheduler instead. (The one cosmetic LoRA we did try mid-stream — Krea 2's krea2_projector_scale — got reverted.)

Why — and the bet we're making

The whole point of the matrix is a telling, comparable number. Pile model-specific LoRAs onto each cell and the step-count, timing and quality verdicts stop being about the model and start being about whoever stacked the better LoRAs. Bare is fair.

The bet baked into that choice: LoRAs won't shift the timing or quality rankings much. That is very much "yet to be seen" — the clean future experiment is to add a realism LoRA to a handful of cells and watch whether the standings reshuffle. The deprecation + per-model machinery would even let us slot LoRA variants in later without disturbing the bare baseline.

For now: bare models, honest numbers, one open question.
