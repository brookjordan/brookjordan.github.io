Tardi on SDXL, the old dog that still knows its ink

2026-10-29 · lesson content

"SDXL for Tardi."

Brook, ranking nine frames for a music video about three towns. Bracknell, with its doomed footbridges and a car park shut for corrosion since 2023, was to look like Jacques Tardi: Adèle Blanc-Sec, the ligne claire revival of the late 1970s, rain on grey streets. The model that won it was the oldest one in the room. SDXL base, from 2023, with a Lightning LoRA bolted on so it finishes before the kettle does.

Hatching, by request, in a prompt that banned hatching

The first try ran on Qwen Image 2512, scene first and style second:

... Drawn in the French ligne claire comic style of Tardi and Floc'h: clean black ink outlines of one even width, no hatching and no cross-hatching, flat areas of muted colour, low contrast, a realistic detailed background, sodium orange and slate grey.

A comic panel, yes. With hatching. The prompt said "no hatching" and then, to be safe, "no cross-hatching". The model heard "comic" and got creative.

Lead with Tardi and keep saying it

The rewrite puts Tardi first, leans hard on the technique, and restates it at the end:

A comic-book panel by Jacques Tardi in the French ligne claire tradition of Hergé and Floc'h. Every shape outlined with a clean black ink line of one constant width; no hatching, no cross-hatching, no shading gradients, no texture; colour laid in completely flat, even areas, muted and low in contrast like an old printed bande dessinée album; clear simple silhouettes; realistic architecture with simplified figures; rain drawn as neat straight ink strokes; flat colours of slate grey, sodium orange and pale ochre. [A concrete footbridge over the ring road in Bracknell at dusk, a closed multi-storey car park behind.] The whole image is a flat-coloured ligne claire comic panel: crisp even outlines and flat colour only. Not a photograph, not painterly, no gradients, no 3D.

SDXL draws Tardi, and Paris with him

Look at it! Grey-blue facades, an ink line with real weight, a rounded old car, one woman walking in the wet. It could be a frame from an album. It took four seconds. FOUR. Eight steps, the Lightning LoRA, sd_xl_base_1.0, and an SDXL negative prompt that says "photograph, 3d render" out loud.

It also ignored the subject. There is no footbridge and no car park. SDXL read "Tardi" and drew Tardi's Paris, which is a lovely picture of the wrong town. The video's Bracknell shots are all bridges and car parks, so those stills get checked one by one.

The other two:

Krea 2 is the purist's pick. It drew the actual footbridge with even outlines and flat colour, very Hergé. Qwen 2.1 painted it like a film backdrop. Brook still chose SDXL for the feel of it, and taste gets a vote.

Then the video did it eleven times

In the music video, every Bracknell shot went the same way. The prompts asked for a brutalist car park, concrete footbridges over a ring road, a man in a flat cap. SDXL handed back this:

Nine of eleven are vintage yellow cars on Haussmann boulevards. The footbridge turns up twice. The car park, never. Lovely, and the wrong town all over again.

Paris was a word-order problem

The prompts opened with the long Tardi paragraph, then the shot's subject, then the cast. SDXL's CLIP encoder reads about 77 tokens at a time and weighs the start of the prompt most, which is why localimagegen already gives the SDXL models the small prompt size. So the subject sat in a later, weaker chunk, and "Tardi" was right at the front pulling in his Paris, cars and all.

For the Bracknell shots, the prompt now runs in a different order. The shot's subject comes first, then the place, as "Bracknell, England, a 1970s concrete new town: a brutalist multi-storey car park and concrete pedestrian footbridges over a wide ring road". A short Tardi line that keeps his name comes after that. There's a negative prompt too: vintage car, classic car, taxi, Paris, Haussmann boulevard, cobbled street.

One catch. A negative prompt does nothing at CFG 1, and the Lightning recipe runs at CFG 1, because guidance only uses the negative when CFG is above 1. So the test had three arms on the same seed: the old prompt, the reordered prompt at CFG 1, and the reordered prompt plus the negative at CFG 1.5.

The top row is the car park shot. The old prompt drew yellow cars by a tower block. Reordered, it draws stacked concrete decks and ramps under a sodium-orange sky. The bottom row is the footbridge shot. The yellow car went, and a long concrete bridge under a deep canopy took its place, in black and white ink. The order did nearly all of it. CFG 1.5 with the negative looks almost the same as CFG 1, with a few fewer stray modern cars.

All thirteen Bracknell shots were re-rendered with the third arm, still SDXL, eight steps, about four seconds each:

BRACKNELL IS BACK. Concrete decks, long footbridges, car park levels, and the observer walking the bridges, still in Tardi's line and flat colour. There are still cars, but now they're 1970s cars in a car park, which is allowed.

One model per illustrator, three different winners. The other rows have their own posts: Sempé on Qwen Image 2.1 and Cassandre on Krea 2.

What still hurts

  • The fix is two shots on one seed, plus Brook's eye on the re-rendered eleven. Order and the negative went in together for the final set, and the test suggests order did most of the work.
  • SDXL with a long style paragraph first loses the subject. For SDXL, put the subject first and keep the style short; for Qwen 2.1 and Krea 2 the style paragraph went first and they held the subject.
  • One seed, one scene, one judge. A ranking, not a measurement.
