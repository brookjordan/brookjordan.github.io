A private Telegram bot, built backwards from honesty

2026-10-05 · telegram-bot architecture reliability

The gallery renders through a CLI that assumes a patient human at a keyboard. The goal here was different: send /schnell a mended patch from a phone and get a validated image back, without the bot ever lying about what happened — and without disturbing the gallery, the Mac mini bot, or anything else sharing this machine and its ComfyUI queue.

The shape

n8n on the NAS owns Telegram identity and a durable request ledger (PostgreSQL, dedicated localimagegen_bot schema). A small authenticated service on this laptop owns the queue, memory admission and job-specific ComfyUI observation. Tailscale connects them; Telegram credentials never reach the laptop. The 15 selected models each get a command; frozen steps and canvas live in one versioned registry that a test keeps byte-compatible with the gallery config.

The constraints that shaped everything

ComfyUI is shared, so the bot owns almost nothing. It cannot interrupt, free memory, clear the queue, or jump it. It observes the real queue and waits behind existing work — a bot-local lock reserves nothing against other clients. Every failure path is tested to prove zero calls to /interrupt, /free or a queue clear.

A POST that loses its response is not a failure. ComfyUI may have accepted the prompt. The job goes to submission_unknown, and reconciliation binds the job to at most one exact prompt by client identity plus graph fingerprint. Zero or two candidates means a human looks, not a retry. The same discipline governs Telegram sends: an ambiguous delivery is stored as uncertain and resending is an explicit operator act. Nothing in this stack is exactly-once, and nothing claims to be.

Memory estimates are not capacity guarantees. Admission samples a fresh, provenance-checked probe and requires an identity-matched measured profile to auto-admit. Missing, stale, implausible or polluted evidence — including the /system_stats fallback that dresses free RAM up as MPS allocation — means the human gets a five-minute consent button instead of a silent gamble. One flagged conflict does not authorise removing a recipe's negative prompt; v1 removes nothing.

Native Telegram ingress acknowledges before we persist. The ledger's unique index narrows the loss window but cannot close it. Production either accepts that explicitly or gets a durable-ingress adapter; what it does not get is a completion report claiming durability the API cannot provide.

Choices worth recording

Steps came from data, not vibes. Each command runs the highest step rung whose measured median render stays under 60 seconds — derived from the _t<n>s_ durations already recorded in ~1,000 committed master filenames. That moved three models off the plan's guesses (krea2 8→2, pixartsigma 20→15, qwen2512 8→3) and left eleven unchanged.

The Gemma converter ships behind a flag. Booru-dialect models (noobai, noobaivpred, pony) are blocked at ingress until the abliterated Gemma 4 E2B candidate passes real fixture qualification; their _raw variants and all twelve native-prompt models work from day one. A bad converter blocks conversion, never rendering — and no fallback silently picks raw for you.

The recipe is frozen, never re-authored. Every bot graph is the gallery's own buildGraph() output, asserted byte-for-byte in tests, with a job-scoped output prefix. Provenance records the effective conditioning texts, workflow hash, model files and seed — so any future "why does the bot look different from the gallery?" has a stored answer.

What is deliberately not done

Nothing here has touched the live ComfyUI, Telegram, or the existing bot. 355 unit/integration tests pass against fakes and a disposable Postgres; the first real render through this stack is still ahead, gated on credential rotation, workflow activation and live smoke tests. The plan called that out repeatedly, and the completion checklist makes "all tasks written" explicitly worthless as a done-signal. The honest state is: built, tested offline, waiting for permission to touch the world.
