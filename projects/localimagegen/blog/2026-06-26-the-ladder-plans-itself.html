The ladder plans itself

2026-06-26 · decision architecture

Knowing when to stop gave us a rule for how many steps each model deserves: dense at the bottom, climb to a community-level ceiling, widen the gaps as each step gets pricier. Good rule. One problem: a rule a human pastes into YAML by hand is still a human pasting into YAML by hand. So the next move wrote itself — make the machine apply the rule.

One number per model, not a hand-typed ladder

The plan: delete the hand-enumerated steps: lists from config/gallery.yaml. Each model keeps exactly one new knob — its ceiling: the community max-useful step count, which is also our predicted plateau. The autopilot computes the actual ladder at runtime, per model × scene × seed, from that ceiling plus the render times it has already measured.

The bootstrap problem — "you need timings to set the cadence, but timings only exist after you render" — solves itself: the floor (1, 2, 4) is always rendered first, and that's what supplies each ladder its own clock.

Cadence from real data, not vibes

I didn't want to guess the gap sizes, so I scraped the _tNNs_ durations off all 1,308 masters on disk. The distribution: P10 = 9s, median 50s, P90 = 217s, max a brutal 497s (hello, Chroma). The gap between rungs is a function of how long the rung below it took:

gap(dur) = clamp(round(1 + 4 * sqrt((dur - 9) / (217 - 9))), 1, MAX_GAP)  // MAX_GAP = 4

The sqrt is the whole trick. I tried the obvious "logarithmic" idea first and it was backwards — geometric thresholds make the slowest models the sparsest, which is the opposite of what you want when slow models are exactly the ones where extra steps buy real image. A hard "cut the top 25%" clamp was worse: it pegged the four slowest models at the coarsest gap. The compressed sqrt curve does what I actually meant — longer renders get relatively more in-between rungs, and the growth never runs away:

gap models (at today's medians)
1 sdxl trio
2 flux4b, wan
3 zimage, flux9b, schnell, qwen2512, krea2
4 ovis, longcat, ideogram, chroma

And because the gap is recomputed per rung, a single slow ladder isn't uniformly coarse — it's dense low and only opens up near its expensive top. Chroma's own numbers: step 4 is 89s → gap 3, but by step 6 (133s) it's gap 4. Cheap where it's cheap, sparse only where it hurts. MAX_GAP = 4 (not 5) because Chroma and Ideogram are the only two slow enough to hit the cap, and they're precisely where I don't want to be coarsest. One constant; bump it back to 5 to relax.

Reuse, don't redo — and never delete

Here's the part that makes it safe to point at a year of legacy masters: existing rungs are anchors, not obstacles. A rung within one gap of where we want one? Reused. We only render the actual holes. So two seeds with different legacy ladders both converge to "enough up to the ceiling" while re-rendering nothing, and an off-grid legacy rung is just kept as a harmless extra — same deprecate-don't-delete spirit. Run it twice, it renders zero the second time. That's the bar.

The plateau always shows, and nothing hugs it

Two niceties fell out of staring at edge cases. The ceiling/plateau is a hard anchor — always rendered, even when it's off the gap grid (at 19, wanting 24, ceiling 20 → we render 20). And the run-up rung sits in the middle, never hugging the plateau: 14 → 20 gives 14, 17, 20, never the wasteful 14, 19, 20.

The gallery tells the truth by default

Last piece, in the web app: show rungs up to the ceiling by default, so the gallery quotes you a realistic max render time. A "show overshoots" toggle reveals the rungs past it. We keep those overshoots — we rendered them on purpose, and on fast models they're a cheap "see, more steps doesn't help" datapoint — we just don't make them the headline.

The cadence isn't a number I picked. It's the shape of the data we already had. The ladder draws itself; I just read it the curve.
