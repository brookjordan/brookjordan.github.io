The kingdom scene — one prompt, three token budgets

2026-06-25 21:36 · content decision

The most ambitious scene yet: a fortified mountaintop kingdom meant to stress crowds, architecture, layered biomes and lighting all at once. Requirements accreted over several iterations:

  • Layered mountainside: grassy lower slopes -> rocky crags + waterfalls -> snowy peaks.
  • Crowds with roles: monks, soldiers, merchants, peasants, royalty; plus animals.
  • Tiered flooded rice terraces with farmers; a blue fjord with small boats.
  • A citadel: castle keep + walled monastery + pagoda, "precisely laid grey stone".
  • Low clouds clinging to the valleys (added in a later pass — easy to forget).
  • A forced golden-hour sky: orange low on the horizon, violet overhead, wispy cirrus, a prominent-but-not-oversized low sun, plus a pale moon and a couple of planets.

How it actually went down (the brainstorm)

This scene wasn't designed so much as yelled into existence over a string of "oh, and…" messages:

"OH! And waterfalls… and rice fields in those tiered layers? With farmers working in them."

"You know what I missed? We want some low clouds in here…"

"OOOOH! Specific time of day! … the sky full of purples, oranges, whispy clouds and a huge sun (not TOO big… but prominent)… maybe include the moon and some planets too."

Every addition made the prompt longer — which forced the next realisation:

"Work out how to distill as much of this as possible for the models that don't support so many tokens, so they get the essence without the detail."

…which is the exact cue for the token-budget tiers.

The real lesson: token budgets

No single prompt fits every model. CLIP text encoders cap around ~77 tokens, while T5/UMT5 and LLM-based encoders take far more. Cram the full paragraph into a CLIP model and the tail just gets truncated. So kingdom ships as three tiers:

  • s — the essence in ~77 tokens (sdxlbase / realvisxl / juggernaut).
  • m — a fuller paragraph (schnell / chroma / wan).
  • l — the maximal, highly-detailed version (flux / qwen / ovis / …).

This single scene is what justified the whole tiering system. It's why the config's prompt resolution is per-model override > tier (s/m/l) > "all": most scenes only need all, but kingdom needs distinct s/m/l text. Distilling one scene to three lengths without losing the essence is a repeatable skill — reorder to cut duplication, and drop "pomp" (epic/cinematic adjectives) before you drop content (the actual objects).
