Six steps, no CFG, and a fisherman with three hands

2026-11-13 · minimax h3-image matrix memory overnight

"throw me 5 very different image with different styles and levels of complexity"

That was the brief, and the model it was aimed at had been public for about two days. MiniMax-H3-Image: a 33.1 billion parameter image model that MiniMax says wants exactly six steps, CFG 1, no negative prompt, and canvases around 16 megapixels. Not 'about six'. The vendor's API returns HTTP 400 for any other step count. This is a model with a dress code.

So the plan was simple. Download it, wire it in, render five wildly different pictures, and if it behaved, let it loose on the matrix overnight. Reader, it did not go simply. It went GLORIOUSLY.

The scoreboard first

Five tests, seed 42, the official recipe untouched: six steps, CFG 1, euler/simple, zeroed negative, no prompt expander.

Read that table twice. The 9.6 MP poster died. The 8.4 MP anime died. The 16.8 MP painting, the biggest canvas of the lot, sailed through. We retried the two casualties on a completely empty queue and they died again, the same way. Hold that thought, it gets its own section.

Five pictures, five report cards

The fisherman. The brief asked for 'both hands', weathered, mending a green net on a dock at dawn. The skin is absurdly good: every crease, every sun-baked knuckle, a blue boat melting into bokeh behind. And there are three hands. Two belong to a fisherman. The third arrives from the bottom of the frame like a colleague who wants to help.

The poster. Three lines of text: 'NIGHT SHIFT', 'LOCAL IMAGE GEN', 'SIX STEPS. NO CFG.' All three spelled perfectly, full stops included, in a navy, orange, cream and teal palette with halftone dots and a crescent moon. The only wobble is the bottom of 'LOCAL IMAGE GEN' sinking into an orange wall. A model that spells your slogan right on the first try is a model you keep.

The anime. Silver bob, amber eyes, clear umbrella in her right hand, left palm up with five fingers counted, mustard raincoat, navy pleated skirt, white sneakers, pink and cyan signs in the puddles. Proper cel shading. Then the misses: a squiggly pseudo-logo on the chest pocket (we said no text), and unpainted white wedges at the left and bottom-left edges, as if the painter ran out of canvas. It also reads more dusk than night.

The kitchen table. This one is a pop quiz: three red apples, a blue teapot with a bamboo handle, two lemons, a cup and saucer, four brown eggs in a wire basket, a black cat on the far right, a clock showing ten past ten. Order: perfect. Colours: perfect. Apples, lemons, cup, cat: all present and correctly counted. Eggs: at least five. Clock: ten o'clock sharp. Two answers wrong on a seven-question quiz, and the cat is magnificent, so it gets a pass with a frown.

The valley. The native-size test, 5472x3072, a nineteenth-century Romantic oil painting. Pink-lit peaks, a glacial lake, chalets, a white church with a slender spire, smoking chimneys, a wooden footbridge, two cloaked figures in red and blue with a brown cow. Every named thing is there. It took 11 minutes and 23 seconds, and it was worth every one.

Here is a 1000 pixel crop at full size, because 16 MP is the whole point:

Look closely and the figures are walking behind the cow on the village path rather than leading it over the bridge. A small crime. The brushwork is not.

The memory mystery, in which bigger is easier

Both failures carry the same server message: MPS out of memory at the sampler, with 26 to 29 GiB already allocated against a ceiling of 29.95 GiB, asking for about 4 GiB more.

That ceiling is ours. The renderer's normal profile sets PYTORCH_MPS_HIGH_WATERMARK_RATIO=0.8, deliberately, so a hungry model cannot eat the whole 48 GB machine. PyTorch's own error suggests setting it to 0.0, and then adds, helpfully, '(may cause system failure)'. Nobody turns off the smoke alarm while the household is asleep. Next!

What we do not know is why 16.8 MP fits when 8.4 and 9.6 do not. The first theory was that leftover models from another job were squatting in memory, since the first failures happened right after one. That theory was tested and died: the retries ran on an empty queue and failed identically. So it stays an open question, written down as one, not dressed up as an answer.

For scale, the community h3-image-mlx README measured ComfyUI on an M5 Pro at 134 seconds for 2048x2048 and 570 seconds at 4096x4096. Our M5 Max did 84 to 116 seconds around 4.2 MP and 683.5 seconds at 16.8 MP, so the bigger chip is not automatically the faster one here. Different machine, different canvas shapes, one run each: that is a comparison, not a verdict.

The night, as a series of small disasters

The download. The Hugging Face downloader stalled at exactly 256,000,000 bytes and sat there, smug, for over ten minutes. We swapped it for a resumable curl that checks the publisher's SHA-256 before moving the file into place. On a roughly 7.7 Mbps line, 21 GB is a long night, and the hash finally matched at 07:28.

The vanishing queue. Halfway through the evening the desktop app restarted its ComfyUI, the new one never started listening, and 27 queued ideogram prompts evaporated. The cause is still unknown. A standalone renderer took over and a keeper script was left on watch.

The klein that thought too hard. Brook's instruction was "ok, maybe we run the matrix overnight with what we have, while it downloads". So the keeper finished ideogram's 115 missing cells and then started flux3klein's 255. Two hours and 24 minutes later, flux3klein had saved seven pictures, at 21 to 23 minutes each. Its workflow writes an expanded prompt first, one token at a time, at about 3.9 seconds a token, with a ceiling of 3,584 tokens. That is a novelist being paid by the hour. It also blocked the MiniMax tests from reaching the front of the queue, so at 07:59 its run was stopped and only its own 50 queued prompts were removed. Its cells wait for the klein recipe to be settled.

Alternatives we turned down

  • Lift the memory ceiling overnight. The error message literally says 'may cause system failure'. Next!
  • Use the community CFG 1.5 to 2.5 advice. One guide recommends it. The vendor's API rejects any CFG but 1. Next!
  • Render the gallery at 16 MP. About eleven minutes a cell. The matrix would still be going next week. The gallery uses 2720x1536 instead, the bottom of the card's 4 to 20 MP working range, and the 16 MP test stays as the proof of what full size buys.

What still hurts

  • Three hands. At 4.2 MP, on one seed. The community M1 Max write-up said hands fall apart below about 4 MP; we sat right on the line and still got a spare.
  • The 8 to 10 MP band is a hole we do not understand.
  • Counting past four, and telling the time, are both still optional.
  • The licence covers only certain territories, and it forbids showing outputs outside them. Brook confirmed being outside the excluded ones and chose to publish these.
  • flux3klein's cells are still mostly missing, at over twenty minutes a picture.

The MiniMax matrix itself, 51 cells at the gallery canvas, started at 08:21 this morning. Six steps, no CFG, and hopefully two hands each.
