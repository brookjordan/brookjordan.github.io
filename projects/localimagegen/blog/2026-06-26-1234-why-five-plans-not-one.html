Why we have five plans, not one

2026-06-26 12:34 · decision architecture milestone

Five plan files turned up in .claude/plans/. Fair question, and I asked it myself: isn't one enough? Shouldn't these be merged?

Short answer: no — and the reason is the same reason you don't keep your finished blueprints, your to-do list, and three design docs in a single Google Doc. They're at different altitudes and different stages of life.

The one that's basically done

localimagegen_build_system is the OG spec — the "extract the scattered Python sweeps into one config-driven Node repo" blueprint. And it's done, sanded smooth. The proof is the gallery that filled itself overnight: 1308/1308 cells, lossless-WebP masters, SSIM-gated compression, a live deploy, the never-idle autopilot. The plan file's checkboxes actually lag reality now — half of them shipped under other posts. That's the tell of a finished spec: it's become a historical artifact, not a worklist.

You don't dissolve a foundation into the walls. You leave it documented and build on top.

The backlog it spawned

The moment the core shipped, the leftover ambitions didn't evaporate — they pooled into a backlog, localimagegen_next_steps: recap the step ladders, build a lig mem profiler, harden the overnight loop, sweep up some cleanups. That's a different altitude from the spec: a flat list of "what's next," not an architecture.

The three that grew teeth

Then a funny thing happened. Three backlog items turned out to have real depth, and each budded off into its own focused plan:

  • The step-ladder recap grew from "edit some numbers by hand" into a whole self-calibrating planner — the ladder that plans itself.
  • "Stall resilience + notifications" grew into a coherent failure taxonomy and a phone-home notifier — the robot phones home.
  • A throwaway one-liner inside that resilience plan — everything gets a timestamp — was big enough (it's a convention, not a feature) to deserve its own page.

Each of those plans literally says "supersedes the stub in next_steps" at the top. That's the merge that actually matters: one idea, one source of truth, with provenance — not a 600-line monolith where a done spec, a backlog, and three live deep-dives elbow each other for the same scroll position.

So what should change

Not a grand merge — a tidy-up. There's genuine duplication to kill: the stall-timeout currently lives in both next_steps and the resilience plan. The resilience plan already claims it, so next_steps should point, not repeat. The right end state is next_steps slimmed to an index — a one-line pointer to each spun-out plan — and build_system left alone as the closed blueprint it is.

One plan would've been tidy on day one and a swamp by day five. Five plans, cross-linked and honest about what supersedes what, is the structure that survives. Now — let's go build the four that aren't done yet.
